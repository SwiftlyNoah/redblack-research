\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Paper~I formalized heads-up Red/Black and showed that its reachable
beliefs factorize over a class prior into two per-seat tilts. This paper
solves the abstraction $\gp$ that forgets the tilts: Red/Black
with beliefs reset to the class prior at every round boundary. The solve
decomposes the game into $5{,}721$ subgame-bearing public classes, orders
them by a layer index, and runs a vectorized form of CFR+ over each class
against solved successors, to per-class exploitability tolerances
of $10^{-5}$ to $10^{-4}$; the value of $\gp$ is $+0.0245$ to the opener,
a $51.22\%$ win probability, and the solve runs in about two hours on a
laptop. The second half measures what the abstraction costs, and returns
three results we did not anticipate. First, an earlier pass of the same
computation converged by every internal meter while carrying values wrong
by up to $0.11$, a hundred times its tolerance; the error was invisible
to convergence checks by construction and was caught only by a
seat-symmetry identity, which the solve of record then enforced exactly.
A later audit (Section~\ref{sec:colourswap}) showed that enforcement
hid rather than fixed the underlying indeterminacy: the colour-swap
automorphism, which the solver never enforces, is violated by the same
profile at the same magnitude, because CFR+ selects arbitrarily among
equilibrium decompositions and the bootstrap composes that selection
noise. A third-generation solve replaces the per-class selection with
the unique equilibrium of an entropy-regularized game, annealed toward
zero, and passes the colour-swap gate at solve precision on every
class, with the regularization's cost stated rather than hidden.
Second, the solved profile deliberately loses contests it could contest,
flipping its own known off-color card with probability up to $0.97$ at
some information sets; we prove this action is simultaneously optimal in
$\gp$ and dominated in the real game $\game$, so the divergence between a
game and its abstraction is not hypothetical but on-path and measurable.
Third, the cost of the belief reset is certified from below: a local
best-response probe beats the exactly-solved profile by at least $0.598$
per game (of a $2.0$ range), and the reset destroys $0.84$ of the public
posterior in reach-weighted total variation. Solving an abstraction
exactly is not solving the game; measuring the difference precisely is
what makes the next step, carrying beliefs, well-posed. Every number is
regenerated by scripts accompanying the papers.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Heads-up Red/Black, formalized in Paper~I, is a two-player zero-sum game of
hidden information: a shared $52$-card deck of $26$ red and $26$ black, five
cards per seat, and rounds of bid, raise, challenge, and procurement in which
the loser of each contest discards a card. Write $\game$ for this game. Paper~I
counted it exactly, at $5.3\times10^{34}$ public decision histories and
$2.4\times10^{33}$ perfect-recall information sets, and proved that at every
round boundary the reachable public posterior over the seats' private types
factorizes as a strategy-independent class prior $\lambda_\kappa$ times two
per-seat tilt vectors (Paper~I, Thm.~10.3). This paper solves the abstraction
that forgets the tilts.

\begin{definition}[The abstraction $\gp$; Paper~I, Def.~10.6]
\label{def:gprime}
$\gp$ is the game identical to $\game$ within each round, except that at
every round boundary of public class $\kappa$ the joint law over type
pairs is reset to the class prior $\lambda_\kappa$; equivalently, the
tilts $g_0$ and $g_1$ of the factorization are replaced by $1$.
\end{definition}

The abstraction is forced. At $2.4\times10^{33}$ information sets there is no
regret table for $\game$, and any tabular method must summarize. The public
class of Paper~I summarizes knowledge exactly (it pins the support of the
posterior; Paper~I, Thm.~10.1) but leaves the shape on that support
history-dependent. Resetting to the class prior makes an exact tabular solve
possible: since every round removes exactly one card (Paper~I, Thm.~4.7), the
classes form a layered directed acyclic graph, and $\gp$ decomposes into
per-class subgames solvable by backward induction over layers.

The first half of the paper carries out that solve. The second half measures
what it means, and three of its results surprised us.

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep3pt
  \item An internally converged solve of $\gp$, green on every
        convergence meter at tolerance $10^{-3}$, carried values wrong
        by up to $0.114$, a hundred times its tolerance. The error was
        invisible to round-local convergence checks by construction and
        was caught only by a seat-symmetry identity
        (Section~\ref{sec:autopsy}), which the second-generation solve
        then enforced exactly. Enforcement blinded that meter, and the
        colour-swap automorphism, which nothing enforces, later read
        violations of the same magnitude on the enforced solve
        (Section~\ref{sec:colourswap}); the third-generation solve
        removes the cause instead of the symptom.
  \item The exactly solved profile plays, with probability up to $0.97$
        at some information sets, an action that flips its own known
        off-color card and loses a contest it could have contested. We
        prove the action is simultaneously optimal in $\gp$ and
        dominated in $\game$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:suicide}): the
        divergence between the game and its abstraction is on-path and
        measurable, not hypothetical.
  \item The abstraction's real-game cost is certified from below. A
        local best-response probe beats the solved profile by at least
        $0.598$ per game of a $2.0$ range
        (Section~\ref{sec:distance}), and the belief reset destroys
        $0.842$ of the public posterior in reach-weighted total
        variation.
\end{enumerate}

The constructive contributions are the decomposition (boundary placement,
private-state structure, row-level accounting), a vector-form CFR+ solver whose
second-generation version solves all of $\gp$ on a laptop in about two hours,
and measurement instruments with explicitly stated scopes, two of which certify
statements about $\game$ itself. Section~\ref{sec:prelim} imports Paper~I's
results and states CFR+; Sections~\ref{sec:decomp}--\ref{sec:discussion} give
the decomposition, the solver, the meters, the results, and what they establish.
Heads-up ($N=2$) throughout, hand size $H=5$, notation continuous with Paper~I.

\subsection{CFR+}\label{sec:cfr}

We use counterfactual regret minimization in its CFR+ variant, and state it as
known material. \emph{Regret matching} \cite{hart2000} plays each action of a
repeated decision with probability proportional to its clipped cumulative
regret, $\sigma^{T+1}(a) \propto [\reg^{T}(a)]^{+}$, uniform when all regrets
are non-positive, and guarantees $\max_a \reg^{T}(a) = O(\sqrt{T})$. \emph{CFR}
\cite{zinkevich2007} extends this to extensive-form games: at each information
set $\infoset$ of player $i$ it regret-matches on the \emph{counterfactual}
regrets, computed from $v_i(\infoset) = \sum_{h\in\infoset}\pi_{-i}(h)\,u_i(h)$,
the expected payoff weighted by opponent-and-chance reach only. Dropping the
player's own reach makes the per-infoset regrets decompose: total regret is
bounded by their sum, so minimizing locally minimizes globally, and in a
two-player zero-sum game a pair of average strategies with average regrets at
most $\varepsilon$ is a $2\varepsilon$-equilibrium. \emph{CFR+}
\cite{tammelin2014,tammelin2015} accelerates CFR by flooring cumulative regrets
at zero after every update, weighting the strategy average linearly in $t$, and
alternating player updates; it is the variant that essentially solved heads-up
limit hold'em \cite{bowling2015}. The output of a run is the \emph{average}
strategy; the current strategy need not converge at all, and every value, meter,
and exported policy in this paper refers to the average.

\begin{remark}[Three caveats that shape this paper]\label{rem:caveats}
First, the guarantee is about average regret, not uniqueness: where the
equilibrium set is large, two runs can converge to different, equally
valid answers, and the type-conditional values this paper bootstraps on
are exactly such objects (Remark~\ref{rem:vnotunique}). Second, the
guarantee is for the game the algorithm actually ran on: we run CFR+ on
a decomposition of Red/Black, so small regret bounds exploitability in
$\gp$, and transferring any claim to $\game$ requires a separate
measurement. Third, the regret decomposition assumes perfect recall,
which holds within a round subgame but fails across boundaries by
construction, since a successor is entered through a class that has
forgotten the bid history.
\end{remark}

\section{The Decomposition}\label{sec:decomp}

\subsection{Subgames and the boundary}\label{sec:subgames}

\begin{definition}[Round subgame]\label{def:subgame}
The subgame rooted at a non-terminal class $(\ell,s_0,s_1)$ consists of:
the pending discarder's joint (drop, reorder) choice; the other seat's
reorder; the bidding phase; and procurement, ending at the resolution
\emph{before} the next discard.
\end{definition}

The rules order a round reorder $\to$ bid $\to$ procure $\to$ discard, so the
segment between two boundaries reads discard $\to$ reorder $\to$ bid $\to$
procure, with the discard first. Terminal classes, whose pending discarder holds
a single card, have no subgame: the forced discard eliminates that seat and the
value is $\pm1$. The root is the opposite special case: $\ell=-1$, no discard is
pending, the pre-stage is just the two reorders, and the bidding starter is seat
$0$ by convention rather than $1-\ell$.

\subsection{Why the boundary sits at procurement resolution}
\label{sec:boundary}

An earlier design placed the boundary at the end of the round, so subgames ran
reorder $\to$ bid $\to$ procure $\to$ discard, matching the rules' own phrasing.
That choice is wrong in a way no round-local check could see. The counterexample
is small: a seat holding one card, known red, first to act against a seat
holding a known red and a known black. By a two-stage matching-pennies argument
the true value of this position is exactly $25\%$ to the first seat; the
end-of-round decomposition priced it at $15.5\%$. With the discard at the end of
a subgame, the loser's keep-secret decision (which card to keep) falls in one
subgame while every response to it falls in the next. The successor, solved
standalone against a class prior, committed to near-pure responses conditioned
on the opponent's type, and the parent's discard could then choose the card that
beats that frozen book. The real game forbids the exploit, because the response
cannot condition on a secret it has not seen: the keep-secret and the response
are effectively simultaneous. Round-local exploitability was small throughout,
and composed best-response brackets confirmed the $15.5\%$ tightly, but within
the belief-reset game; only an oracle refusing the reset could see the
$9.5$-point error.

The fix moves the boundary one discard earlier, so the keep-secret decision and
every response to it live inside a single exactly solved subgame. That is
Definition~\ref{def:subgame}, and it is why the pending discarder's drop and
reorder form one joint private choice (Definition~\ref{def:privstate}):
separating them would let the reorder condition on a drop the opponent never
sees, the same leak in miniature. The general lesson is that a decomposition
boundary must never cut a coupling between moves the real game makes at the same
time. Where it does, each side is solved against a frozen model of the other,
and the error can be large while every meter computed inside the decomposition
reports success.

\subsection{Information sets, types, and private states}\label{sec:info}

\begin{definition}[Information set]\label{def:infoset}
Fix a class $(\ell,s_0,s_1)$ and a seat $i$. An information set of seat
$i$ inside that class is a pair (public node, seat $i$'s private state),
where the public node is everything both players can see (the class, the
bid chain so far, and the flip counters with the colors revealed this
contest) and the private state is everything seat $i$ knows that its
opponent does not.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Private type]\label{def:type}
Seat $i$'s type at a boundary is the pair $(r,x)$: the number of reds in
its pre-discard hand, and the number of reds among the cards it has
itself discarded. The public bounds truncate the first coordinate to
$\rlb^{\,i}\le r\le n_i-\blb^{\,i}$; the second is invisible to the
opponent.
\end{definition}

Discarded cards can never be flipped, so $x$ cannot affect any payoff directly,
but the deck is finite: a player who has seen $k$ reds among their own five
dealt cards should assign an unseen card probability $(26-k)/47$ of being red,
from $0.447$ to $0.553$ as $k$ runs from $5$ to $0$. So $x$ is private
information about the deck rather than the hand, droppable under an idealized
infinite deck and retained because the game is played with $52$ cards.

\begin{definition}[Private state]\label{def:privstate}
For a seat that is not the pending discarder, a private state is an
ordering of its $(r,\,n_i-r)$ color multiset together with $x$, one per
admissible ordering, of which there are $\binom{n_i}{r}$. For the
pending discarder, the drop and the reorder are a single joint private
choice: a private state is (drop color $\times$ ordering of the
surviving $n_i-1$ cards, $x'$), where $x'=x+1$ if the dropped card was
red and $x'=x$ otherwise. Two paths reaching the same post-discard hand
(dropping a red from $r$ reds versus a black from $r-1$) remain distinct
states, because they imply different $x'$ and therefore different
beliefs about the deck.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Counting states and rows in one class]\label{ex:rows}
Take the class $\ell=0$, $s_0=(2,0,0)$, $s_1=(1,0,0)$. Seat $0$ has
made $H-n_0=3$ discards, so $x\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ and $r\in\{0,1,2\}$; its
joint (drop, ordering) choices per $r$ are $1,2,1$, so
$\text{states}_0=(1+2+1)\times4=16$. Seat $1$ has $4$ discards,
$x\in\{0,\dots,4\}$, $r\in\{0,1\}$, one ordering of a single card:
$\text{states}_1=10$. The in-round sizes are $(1,1)$: $7$ bid nodes
($3$ acted by seat $1$, $4$ by seat $0$, hence
$3\times10+4\times16=94$ rows) and $14$ procurement nodes
($8\times10+6\times16=176$ rows unmasked, of which the consistency
mask removes $26$, leaving $150$). Total: $94+150=244$ rows.
\end{example}

\subsection{Decision nodes and rows}\label{sec:rows}

Within a round, the public structure is the round automaton of Paper~I on the
in-round sizes $(n_0',n_1')$. Two kinds of node carry decisions: \emph{bid
nodes} (color, chain), plus the pre-bid node, $2^{\,T'+1}-1$ in all, with the
actor alternating along the chain; and \emph{procurement nodes} (color, chain,
$x$, $y$), where $x,y$ count cards already flipped off the bidder's and
challenger's decks and only the bidder acts. By Paper~I's order-irrelevance
lemma the procurement nodes form a lattice on the two counters, not a tree of
flip sequences. Resolutions are terminal within the round; they carry values,
not decisions.

\begin{definition}[Row]\label{def:row}
A row is one (decision node, private state) pair belonging to the seat
that acts at that node. Each row holds a cumulative-regret vector and a
cumulative-strategy vector, one entry per legal action.
\end{definition}

The row count is the size of the regret table and the primary determinant of
memory and time. It is not a simple product of nodes and states: different nodes
are acted on by different seats, and procurement rows whose flipped prefix could
not have matched the bid color are masked out entirely. Table~\ref{tab:rowscale}
gives the totals by layer; they are structural, derivable without solving
anything.

\begin{remark}[A lossless order-class reduction]\label{rem:orderclass}
Those counts enumerate hand orderings in full, though only an
ordering's leading run is ever observable (procurement halts at the
first off-color card), so the $\binom{r+b}{r}$ orderings of an $(r,b)$
multiset collapse losslessly to $r+b$ order classes. The reduction
removes about $12\%$ of private states and, because the saving grows
with depth ($0\%$ at $T'\le4$, $31\%$ at the root), roughly $19\%$ of
the total row count: some $62$M of $336$M, $40$M of it at $T'=9$. The
first-generation solver did not take it; the second-generation solver
does (Section~\ref{sec:gen2}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The root is large and cheap]\label{rem:rootcheap}
The root has the largest public structure anywhere ($2{,}047$ bid
nodes, $65{,}790$ procurement nodes) yet only $815{,}456$ rows, about
four average layer-$9$ classes, because nobody has discarded: $x=0$
for both seats and $\sum_r\binom{5}{r}=2^5=32$ private states each.
The public game shrinks with $T'$ but the private state space grows
with the number of discards, and the second effect dominates.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Entry beliefs and the reset}\label{sec:entrybeliefs}

A subgame cannot be solved without a distribution over the type pairs it starts
from. We use the \emph{bounded hypergeometric} law: treat both hands as drawn
from the residual $26/26$ budget and renormalize over the region the public
bounds allow. This is the class prior $\lambda_\kappa$, and it is the one
modeling choice in the design. It differs from the exact posterior: the class
determines the support of the posterior exactly (Paper~I, Thm.~10.1), but the
shape on that support depends on how the opponent has been playing, and
cross-round bid history is discarded outright. Solving every subgame against
$\lambda_\kappa$ is what makes the solved object $\gp$
(Definition~\ref{def:gprime}) rather than $\game$: CFR+'s guarantees apply to
$\gp$, and in $\game$ the profile is a candidate strategy whose exploitability
must be measured. The boundary counterexample of Section~\ref{sec:boundary}
shows the resulting error need not be small; that is why
Section~\ref{sec:meters} exists.

\subsection{Layered induction and bootstrapping}\label{sec:induction}

Every resolution of a subgame at layer $T'$ identifies a successor class at
layer $T'-1$: apply the realized discard's decay to the entry bounds, raise them
to the colors seen this round, and record the new loser. Because $T'$ strictly
decreases (Paper~I, Thm.~4.7), the classes form a layered DAG and are solved
bottom-up, each layer's answers serving as terminal payoffs for the layer above.

\begin{definition}[Type-conditional value matrix]\label{def:vtypes}
Write $m_i$ for the number of seat $i$'s types. The solution of a class
returns a matrix $V\in\mathbb{R}^{m_0\times m_1}$, where $V[t_0,t_1]$ is
the seat-$0$ value when the seats hold types $t_0,t_1$ and both play the
solved average strategy.
\end{definition}

A parent's resolution looks up its successor class and reads the entry of $V$
matching the types the seats hold at that moment. Only $V$ is needed to solve a
parent, so high layers solve against a values-only store rather than full child
solutions.

\begin{remark}[$V$ is not unique]\label{rem:vnotunique}
Different equilibria of the same subgame can produce different $V$ while
agreeing on the aggregate value. Where a private coordinate is
payoff-irrelevant but belief-relevant, and $x$ is exactly this, the
solver may split a mixed strategy into near-pure branches indexed by
that coordinate, and the individual entries of $V$ are then arbitrary
across the equilibrium set. The aggregate is pinned; the cells are not.
This is a measurement trap, and Sections~\ref{sec:gen2}
and~\ref{sec:meters} return to it.
\end{remark}

\section{The Solver}\label{sec:solver}

\subsection{Vector form}\label{sec:vector}

Walking the game tree one node at a time, as textbook CFR does, is a poor fit
here: a seat's private states are numerous and structurally identical, each
facing the same public node with the same legal actions. Instead we hold, at
each public node, a matrix whose rows are the acting seat's private states and
whose columns are the legal actions, and an iteration becomes a sequence of
dense matrix operations over public nodes. Per class, for each seat $i$:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Object & Shape & Meaning\\
\midrule
\code{states} & $P_i$ & private states (Definition~\ref{def:privstate})\\
\code{type\_idx} & $P_i$ & type of each private state\\
\code{regret[node]} & $P_i \times A_{\text{node}}$ & cumulative regret\\
\code{strat\_sum[node]} & $P_i \times A_{\text{node}}$ & cumulative strategy, linearly weighted\\
\code{reach} & $P_i$ & current reach per private state\\
\code{entry\_wt} & $m_0\times m_1$ & bounded-hypergeometric prior\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A private state is not compatible with every public node: if a procurement node
records three flips all matching the bid color, any state whose top three cards
differ is impossible there. Rather than branch, a precomputed boolean mask per
(node, seat) zeroes those rows; masked rows are excluded from the row count. The
row count of Definition~\ref{def:row} is exactly $\sum_{\text{nodes}}
P_{\text{actor(node)}}$ under the masks, which is what Example~\ref{ex:rows}
computed by hand.

One sweep, for the player being updated: (1) regret-match each node's regret
matrix rowwise into a strategy matrix, clipping negatives; (2) propagate reach
forward from the entry weights through the pre-stage choice and down the public
graph, applying strategies and masks; (3) compute counterfactual values at
resolutions by contracting the successor's $V$ against the opponent's reach, and
propagate backward, accumulating per-action values; (4) add (action value $-$
node value), weighted by opponent-and-chance reach, into the regret matrix and
clip at zero; (5) accumulate $t\cdot\pi_{\mathrm{own}}\cdot\sigma$ into the
strategy sum. Then swap players; the average strategy is \code{strat\_sum}
normalized rowwise. Step (5) must run at every information set the updating
player can reach on their own, whether or not the opponent currently reaches it:
gating on opponent reach freezes rows the opponent has temporarily stopped
visiting, leaving early garbage in the average and making it spuriously
exploitable.

\subsection{Cost model}\label{sec:cost}

Time per class scales as rows $\times$ iterations $/$ throughput, with
throughput in row-iterations per second. Rows are structural
(Table~\ref{tab:rowscale}); iterations are what convergence demands
(Section~\ref{sec:results}). Layer $9$ alone holds half the rows, and layers $8$
and $9$ together hold $81.5\%$, so scheduling decisions should be made about
those two layers first.

\subsection{The second-generation solver}\label{sec:gen2}

The first full solve (Section~\ref{sec:autopsy}) ran the machinery above at
$\mathrm{tol}=10^{-3}$ on a cloud fleet. Its autopsy motivated three changes,
each a structural observation about the game rather than an optimization trick,
that together turned a \$23, twelve-hour fleet job into a two-hour laptop job at
$10\times$ to $100\times$ tighter tolerance.

\paragraph{Ordering collapse.} A seat's private state included its chosen deck
ordering, but orderings presenting the same color prefix behavior are
strategically identical: the opponent can only ever observe a prefix.
Ordering-sensitive states therefore quotient into the order classes of
Remark~\ref{rem:orderclass}, the same state-versus-information distinction that
collapses procurement states in Paper~I, shrinking private state spaces by an
order of magnitude at the deep layers.

\paragraph{Seat-mirror reflection.} A class and its seat-swapped image are the
same subgame with the labels exchanged, so only one of each pair is solved and
the other is produced by reflection. Mirror antisymmetry, the first solve's
sharpest measured failure, then holds exactly by construction, and the work
halves. The honest check moves one level up: re-solving a sample of reflected
classes directly and comparing prior-weighted aggregates gives residuals of
$1.5\times10^{-6}$ to $8.9\times10^{-6}$, against first-solve mirror gaps of up
to $0.114$.

\begin{remark}[Equilibrium multiplicity is not error]
\label{rem:multiplicity}
Cell-wise, a re-solved class and its reflection can disagree by as much
as $6.7\times10^{-2}$ in individual type-pair conditional values while
agreeing to $10^{-6}$ in every prior-weighted aggregate. Zero-sum
equilibria pin values, not solutions, and conditional-value cells are
selection-dependent (Remark~\ref{rem:vnotunique}). Consumers must never
compare cells across independently produced solutions, a rule that
returns with force in Paper~III, where per-type promises are exactly
such cells.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{The kernel.} Profiling showed the solve was interpreter-bound: tiny
dense matrices under huge node counts. Compiling the sweep, and later the
best-response sweeps of Section~\ref{sec:meters}, into a JIT kernel recovered
the arithmetic throughput the cost model assumes. With all three changes the
full game solves locally in $2$h$02$m at layer tolerances of $10^{-5}$ to
$10^{-4}$, and the pipeline is deterministic: re-running it reproduces the solve
bit-for-bit.

\section{Meters}\label{sec:meters}

Each instrument below certifies a different claim, and confusing them is the
main way to overstate a result. The first four are internal to the
decomposition; the last two speak about $\game$ (Table~\ref{tab:meters}).

\subsection{Round-local exploitability}

\begin{definition}[$\varepsilon_{\text{round}}$]
For a single class, hold the solved continuation values fixed and
compute both players' exact best responses against the average profile
within that round. The sum of their gains is the round-local
exploitability.
\end{definition}

This certifies that CFR+ has converged on the subgame given its inherited
values; computed with successor values held fixed, it is blind by construction
to whether those values were right.

\subsection{Composed exploitability}

\begin{definition}[$\varepsilon_{\text{comp}}$]
Best-respond across the whole class DAG, in the round and in every
successor round, against the cached average profile.
\end{definition}

This certifies exploitability in $\gp$, not in Red/Black. Two sub-meters exist:
a \emph{lower bound}, since any concrete deviating strategy's gain bounds the
true best response from below, and an \emph{exact} figure for a redeal variant
in which types are re-drawn at every boundary, where scalar continuations make
backward induction exact. Neither removes the belief reset; they measure inside
it.

\begin{remark}[A negative exploitability reading, 2026-07]
\label{rem:negeps}
An early composed-exploitability run reported
$\varepsilon_{\text{comp}}<0$, impossible for a true best response. The
cause was the lower-bound structure itself: child-level best response
maximizes a child-posterior-weighted total, so a parent can weight
exactly the type pairs sitting below the profile's value, and a
composition of locally best responses is not a globally best response.
The number was a valid lower bound doing what lower bounds do; every
meter is now labeled with its bound direction in the code, and an
``impossible'' reading is treated as a definition surfacing rather than
a bug. Separately, a best-response context was found loading per-state
tables into per-shared-row storage, misaligning every class where the
discarder collapse makes those indexings differ and voiding every
composed figure computed before the fix; the kernel best response is
now validated against an independent reference implementation on
$1{,}184$ classes to $|\Delta|\le4.4\times10^{-16}$ before any number
it produces is reported.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The perfect-recall oracle}

\begin{definition}[Oracle bracket]
Build the explicit perfect-recall game tree over the raw engine, with
information sets indexed by a player's full own history and every
public observation, and bracket the value with exact best responses.
\end{definition}

This certifies the true value in the real game, with no belief reset anywhere,
but the tree is explicit, so it is restricted to small classes: it says the most
and scales the worst.

\subsection{The seat mirror}\label{sec:mirror}

A class and its seat-swapped image must have exactly opposite values under any
correct solver: $v(\ell{=}0,A,B) = -v(\ell{=}1,B,A)$. Once classes bootstrap
from other classes, mirror discrepancy becomes a cheap proxy for composition
error, precisely the quantity $\varepsilon_{\text{round}}$ cannot see. It is
an internal instrument both scalable and sensitive to composition, which made
it the practical tripwire for the first solve (Section~\ref{sec:autopsy}).
The second-generation solve enforces the identity by reflection, which makes
this meter read zero by construction; the colour-swap automorphism
(Section~\ref{sec:colourswap}) is the surviving measured instrument of the
same kind, and nothing in any solver generation enforces it.

\subsection{Instruments for the real game}\label{sec:realmeters}

The remaining two instruments certify statements about $\game$ itself, and both
scale. Their target is standard zero-sum exploitability.

\begin{definition}[Real-game exploitability]\label{def:epsg}
For a profile $\sigma=(\sigma_0,\sigma_1)$ of $\game$,
\[
  \epsG(\sigma) \;=\;
  \max_{\sigma_0'} u_0(\sigma_0',\sigma_1)
  \;+\;
  \max_{\sigma_1'} u_1(\sigma_0,\sigma_1'),
\]
the total gain available to best-responding deviators. It is
non-negative, zero exactly at a Nash equilibrium of $\game$, and
measured per game on the value range $[-1,1]$ (a $2.0$ range).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[TV meter]\label{def:tv}
Play the profile against itself. At every boundary history $h$ with
class $\kappa(h)$, compute the exact public posterior $\mu_h$ over type
pairs by a forward pass over the factored belief family of Paper~I
(Thm.~10.3), validated against brute-force replay to $10^{-12}$, and
report the reach-weighted total variation
\[
  \overline{\mathrm{TV}}
  \;=\;
  \mathbb{E}_{h}\Bigl[\,
    \tfrac12 \sum_{t_0,t_1}
    \bigl|\mu_h(t_0,t_1)-\lambda_{\kappa(h)}(t_0,t_1)\bigr|
  \,\Bigr],
\]
the expectation taken over the boundaries visited in self-play, so each
boundary is weighted by the profile's own reach.
\end{definition}

The TV meter measures the size of the modeling choice on the profile's own play
path, where the choice is being made.

\begin{definition}[LBR probe]\label{def:lbr}
A local best responder \cite{lisy2017} plays one seat against the
stored profile. It maintains the exact opponent-type posterior
conditioned on the public history and its own private trajectory,
prices each of its actions by contracting the profile's per-class
continuation values against that posterior, and plays the argmax. Its
measured win-rate gain over the profile-vs-profile baseline (mirrored
seeds, Wilson interval) is a certified lower bound on
$\epsG(\sigma)$.
\end{definition}

The certification comes from the games actually won, not from the derivation, so
it holds no matter how crude the responder is. The probe certifies no upper
bound: a profile it cannot beat is not thereby safe. Upper bounds are
Paper~III's subject.

\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

The abstraction $\gp$ has been solved twice: all $5{,}721$ subgame-bearing
classes, first at $\mathrm{tol}=10^{-3}$ on a cloud fleet, then, after the first
solve's autopsy, with the second-generation solver. The second solve is the
solve of record and is reported first.

\subsection{The solve of record}\label{sec:solve2}

The second-generation solver re-solved the entire game in $2$h$02$m on
a laptop, eight workers, at layer tolerances of $10^{-5}$ to $10^{-4}$,
which is $10\times$ to $100\times$ tighter than the first solve. Root
$\varepsilon_{\text{round}} = 4.9\times10^{-5}$; six classes finished
above their layer tolerance, all within a $10^{-5}$ band of it. Mirror
antisymmetry is exact by construction, and the reflection residual
against independently re-solved mirrors is $1.5\times10^{-6}$ to
$8.9\times10^{-6}$. The pipeline is deterministic and reproduces itself
bit-for-bit.
\[
  v(\text{root}) \;=\; +0.02449 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \mathbf{51.22\%} \text{ to the player who opens.}
\]
This is the value of $\gp$ of record. The oracle bracket agrees with
the solved value to $1.4\times10^{-4}$ on every class small enough to
bracket ($T'\le3$). For scale, the exactly known $T'=2$ endgame gives
its opener $40651/78302\approx51.92\%$ (Paper~I, Rem.~10.5).

\begin{remark}[Which root value, 2026-08]\label{rem:rootvalue}
The $+0.02449$ above is the CFR+ value of this solve (the store
\code{solve-2-kernel}) at its stated tolerance, and later solver
generations bracket how far its digits should be trusted. The
unique-selection annealed ladder of Section~\ref{sec:colourswap}
(the store \code{solve-4-qre-reach}) prices the root at $0.018956$,
a readout carrying the selection bias of its finite final temperature
($\tau_f=3\times10^{-3}$); an unmasked control re-solve under the
identical ladder with the value-based stopping rule of
Section~\ref{sec:stoprule} prices it at $0.018602$, so the stopping
rule's own effect at the root is $-3.5\times10^{-4}$
(\code{scripts/qre\_solve.py} ladder readouts, recorded in the
solve-5 journal). The value of $\gp$ is therefore determined by this
program to about the third decimal, and no fourth-decimal claim
should be read into any single solve's readout.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The first solve and its autopsy}\label{sec:autopsy}

The first solve converged by every internal meter. All $5{,}721$ classes met
$\varepsilon_{\text{round}}\le10^{-3}$ with none hitting the iteration cap
($\mathrm{max\_iters}=6000$); median iterations per layer ran from $100$ to
$475$, U-shaped in depth ($275$ at $T'=2$, $100$ at $T'=5$, $350$ at $T'=9$,
$475$ at the root), so a cost model extrapolated from shallow layers alone
underestimates the deep ones. Fully mixed keep-secret classes needed up to
$\sim\!2{,}700$ iterations against a median of a few hundred; a common default
cap of $800$ would have silently truncated about $17\%$ of the shallowest layer,
and a generous cap is nearly free since the solver exits at tolerance. Layers
$2$ through $9$ ran on a 28-physical-core cloud VM in $43{,}653$\,s ($12.1$
hours) for about \$23; the root, a single unparallelizable class, ran locally in
$2{,}019$\,s ($33.6$ minutes), an Apple M-series performance core measuring
about $2.2\times$ a cloud core. The first solve priced the root at $v=+0.0266$
($51.33\%$).

On the round-local meter the solve was uniformly green. The seat-mirror
identity, which that meter cannot see, was violated across the $5{,}720$ mirror
pairs:

Layer $T'$ & $2$ & $3$ & $4$ & $5$ & $6$ & $7$ & $8$ & $9$\\
\midrule
Worst gap & $0.0002$ & $0.0056$ & $0.0141$ & $0.0288$ & $0.1142$ & $0.0767$ & $0.0495$ & $\mathbf{0.1146}$\\

Median $0.0014$; worst $0.1146$, over $100\times$ that class's own
$\varepsilon_{\text{round}}$ and $5.7$ percentage points of win rate. This is
the failure Section~\ref{sec:meters} predicts: $\varepsilon_{\text{round}}$
certifies a class against its inherited values and is blind to whether those

layer every game enters on its second round. The identity check was the only

carrying about $0.11$ points of composition error, consistent with its own
mirror-gap table.

\begin{remark}[S5-0 refutation, 2026-08]\label{rem:s5refutation}
Call \emph{play-time guards} the deployment rule that refuses weakly
dominated actions (the suicide flip of Theorem~\ref{thm:suicide} and
bids of impossible counts) at play time while leaving the solved
tables untouched, and call the \emph{belief-carrying probe} Paper~III's
instrument: the stored profile is deployed with each round re-solved
under the exactly tracked public posterior, and the LBR probe of
Definition~\ref{def:lbr}, holding a byte-exact shadow of that
resolver, certifies a lower bound on $\epsG$ of the deployed agent.
On that instrument the guards' price is visible and large. For the
solve of record (the store \code{solve-2-kernel}), at $n=3{,}200$
games per arm on matched seeds, the probe certifies
$\epsG \ge +0.0919$ $[+0.0573,\,+0.1262]$ with guards off against
$\epsG \ge +0.0306$ $[-0.0040,\,+0.0652]$ with guards on ($z=2.45$,
$p=0.014$): the guards cut the probe's edge to a third. The
unique-selection store \code{solve-4-qre-reach}
(Section~\ref{sec:colourswap}) moves the same direction
($0.1220 \to 0.0760$) without resolving at its $n=1{,}000$
($z=1.03$, $p=0.30$) and is reported as such. Artifacts
\code{s5\_probe\_solve2\_base*.json} and
\code{s5\_probe\_solve2\_guards*.json}
(\code{scripts/probe\_resolver.py}, pooled by
\code{scripts/pool\_probe.py}). One caveat attaches to the magnitude
rather than the sign: the guards-on probe best-responds against an
unguarded opponent model, and since the guards prune only weakly
dominated actions the sign is theory-safe. These pooled figures are
the $\epsG$ numbers of record for the solve of record. The first
$n=1{,}000$ batch of the chain reproduced the previously published
$0.0780$ bound to four decimals before the extension was bought, and
the batches differ only by seed range; the earlier figure was a
faithful low-$n$ reading of the same quantity, and the pool extends
its sample and adds the guards axis.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The colour-swap audit and the unique-selection re-solve}
\label{sec:colourswap}

Red/Black's deck holds exactly $26$ red and $26$ black cards and no rule
distinguishes the colours, so exchanging red and black everywhere is an
automorphism of the game and of $\gp$ (the class prior is
colour-symmetric). It maps a class by swapping each seat's certain-red
and certain-black bounds, and a private type $(r,x)$ of a seat with
pre-discard hand size $n$ and $d$ discards to $(n-r,\,d-x)$. Because a
two-player zero-sum game has a unique value, the value, the per-type
conditional values, and (for any single equilibrium) nothing at all is
forced about the per-type-pair decomposition; the value and conditionals
must agree exactly between a class and its swapped image, whatever
equilibrium a solver lands on. No solver generation enforces any of
this, which makes it a meter in exactly the sense the enforced seat
mirror no longer is.

\begin{remark}[The audit, 2026-08]
Measured on the second-generation solve of record
(\texttt{scripts/check\_symmetry.py}, artifact
\texttt{symmetry\_full.json}; all $2{,}971$ colour orbits), the
invariant fails almost everywhere above the leaves: the per-cell
decomposition $v_{\text{types}}$ diverges from its swapped image from
layer $T'{=}3$ (worst $0.055$, rising to $1.67$ at $T'{=}8$), per-type
conditional values diverge by up to $0.37$ ($T'{=}9$), and aggregate
class values by up to $0.034$, against reported
$\varepsilon_{\text{round}}$ of $10^{-5}$ to $10^{-4}$. The profile
therefore does not determine the values of $\gp$ to better than about
$0.03$, and its type-conditional advice not to better than about
$0.37$, no matter what the round-local meters say. The cause is not a
colour-dependent defect: re-solving violating orbit pairs against
colour-symmetrised leaf values
(\texttt{scripts/symmetry\_discriminate.py}) reproduces both members
as exact images of each other to $10^{-16}$, while raw leaves
differing by only $10^{-4}$ move cells by $0.14$ to $0.45$. CFR+ pins
the aggregate value but selects arbitrarily among per-type-pair
decompositions; the selection is seeded at swap-invariant classes
(where one solve on exactly symmetric inputs still selects an
asymmetric decomposition, measured at $4.2\times10^{-3}$) and the
class-to-parent bootstrap then amplifies it, because the equilibrium
correspondence is discontinuous in its leaf inputs. Enforcing the seat
mirror in the second-generation solve removed one visible symptom of
this indeterminacy and left the indeterminacy itself untouched, as the
first-generation autopsy predicted enforcement would.
\end{remark}

The third-generation solve removes the freedom instead of a symptom.
Each class is solved to the unique equilibrium of the
entropy-regularized round game: per information set the policy follows
a mirror-descent step
$\ell \leftarrow (\ell + \eta\, q / c) / (1 + \eta\tau)$ on logits
$\ell$, with $q$ the counterfactual action values, $c$ the row's fixed
entry-prior mass, and the temperature $\tau$ annealed down a fixed
geometric ladder from $0.3$ to $10^{-3}$, last iterate throughout
(\texttt{scripts/v9\_solve.py}; the update is magnetic mirror descent
with a uniform magnet under a reweighted dilated entropy, so the
regularized equilibrium is unique for every $\tau>0$). Uniqueness makes
the solution equivariant under every automorphism without enforcing
any: the colour gate stays live, and the seat mirror could be measured
again. The cost is stated rather than hidden: the exact best-response
gap of the final policy is floored by $\tau$ (measured
$4\times10^{-3}$ at $T'{=}4$ up to $0.19$ on the largest $T'{=}9$
class, \texttt{scripts/qre\_calibrate.py}), while the value bias
against the CFR+ value, which is unique and therefore ground truth for
values, is $2.7\times10^{-4}$ to $7.8\times10^{-4}$ at
$\tau_f=10^{-3}$ and scales linearly in $\tau_f$. Policies are full
support everywhere, so the off-path uniform fallback of
Section~\ref{sec:playing} no longer exists, and near-indifferent
actions remain visibly mixed instead of being laundered into
certainties. The re-solve is deterministic end to end; split across
two machines, every one of the $2{,}288$ overlap-checked class values
agreed bitwise.
\begin{remark}[What the third generation bought, 2026-08]
The re-solve completed on all $5{,}721$ classes. Measured on the
colour swap, which nothing in either solver enforces, the worst
disagreement anywhere between a class and its swapped image falls from
$1.67$ to $3.5\times10^{-9}$ in the per-type-pair cells, from $0.37$ to
$3.3\times10^{-10}$ in the type-conditional values, and from
$3.4\times10^{-2}$ to $2.7\times10^{-11}$ in the class values. The
second-generation profile violates the invariant on $2{,}206$ of
$2{,}971$ colour orbits; the third violates it on none beyond a
$10^{-9}$ threshold set tighter than the arithmetic.

The strength cost is nil and the strength gain is also nil. Over
$1{,}500$ mirrored pairs with play-time guards disabled and no
fallbacks on either side, the two profiles are indistinguishable
($0.4970$, $95\%$ CI $[0.479, 0.515]$, $p = 0.76$). A local
best-response probe carrying beliefs to depth $9$, run on both
profiles with identical deals, returns $\epsG \ge 0.0780$
$[0.0160, 0.1394]$ for the second generation and $\epsG \ge 0.1220$
$[0.0601, 0.1829]$ for the third; the difference is not significant
($p = 0.32$) and separating a gap that size would require some
$8{,}100$ games per arm. Both readings are guards-off at
$n=1{,}000$; the pooled $n=3{,}200$ figures of
Remark~\ref{rem:s5refutation}, which reproduce the $0.0780$ batch to
four decimals within the guards-off pool, are the numbers of record.
The honest statement is that unique selection
buys determinacy and costs nothing measurable, not that it plays
better.

One negative result deserves recording because it settles a question
three generations of the certificate program could not. Section~1's
audit raised the possibility that the leaf-price dishonorability of
$0.11$ to $0.26$ measured per resolve was largely the same defect as
the colour inconsistency of $0.11$, rather than a limit of
depth-limited resolving. Removing the inconsistency entirely does not
lower the probe's edge. The limitation is intrinsic.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The value-based stopping rule, and re-arming a meter}
\label{sec:stoprule}

The annealed ladder of Section~\ref{sec:colourswap} originally
stopped each rung on a policy-movement criterion, and
near-saturated policies keep moving long after the values have
settled, so that criterion overpays. The fast variant of the ladder
stops each rung on the value residual: a rung ends when the
class's aggregate value stops moving at rung scale. The change cuts a
production ladder about $30\times$, from $40{,}406$ iterations per
class to about $1{,}300$.

The rule was validated on the full policy-equivariance sweep rather
than a pilot (artifact \code{deps\_value\_stoprule\_full.json},
script \code{scripts/deps\_value\_stoprule.py}): all $360$
sampled colour orbits re-solved under the identical production ladder
with only the stopping rule changed. The meter is $d_\varepsilon$,
the measured exploitability cost of a colour orbit's policy
asymmetry, and an orbit is \emph{harmful} when
$d_\varepsilon>10^{-2}$. The harmful tail falls from $22$ orbits to
$6$ of the $346$ comparable, about $4\times$ fewer, while the worst
magnitude is not reduced ($0.239$, at $T'{=}6$) and five previously
clean orbits became divergent under the new rule. Overall solve
quality is unchanged: taking the worse of each orbit's two twin
solves by single-shot round exploitability, the median moves from
$0.0030$ to $0.0030$ and the p95 from $0.0295$ to $0.0303$, at about
$30\times$ less compute. The honest summary is a reduced problem
rather than a solved one: saturated near-ties still select arbitrary
plateaus per twin, so the colour-swap gate and the per-layer outlier
flags below stay mandatory on any solve run under this rule.

\paragraph{Re-arming a meter, as a worked example.} The solve
pipeline flags a finished class-solve whose single-shot round
exploitability exceeds a per-layer threshold (\code{EPS\_FLAG}); a
separate warning threshold (\code{EPS\_WARN}) triggers an automatic
retry at halved step size before a solve reaches any store. The flag
table had been calibrated on the slow ladder, and the fast ladder's
tail lands elsewhere, so the table was recalibrated before the first
fast-ladder production solve
(\code{scripts/recalibrate\_eps\_flags.py}, artifact
\code{eps\_flag\_recalibration.json}). Two decisions
carry the numbers. First, the per-layer p95 is taken over the
censored sub-\code{EPS\_WARN} population: the validation sweep
applies no retry ladder by design, so its raw distribution holds
divergent single-shot solves ($1.91$ at $T'{=}5$) that production
retries before any store holds them, and a raw p95 would set
thresholds describing solves no store ever contains. Second, the
layers the sweep does not reach ($T'{=}2,3$) were re-solved and
measured directly rather than extrapolated from neighbours: $15$ of
$36$ classes at $T'{=}2$ and $19$ of $132$ at $T'{=}3$ trip
\code{EPS\_WARN} single-shot, so any extrapolation would have been
badly wrong. The recalibration found the old table wrong in both
directions, $5.3\times$ too tight at $T'{=}5$ and $3.3\times$ too
loose at $T'{=}7$. A meter calibrated on one solver regime silently
miscounts on another, in whichever direction that regime's tail
happens to land.

mapping is correct, and every joint discard-and-reorder commitment replayed
exactly. Self-play seat-0 rates bracket the solved root ($51.22\%$) within their
intervals. The deployed profile is the concealment tie-break variant:
value-neutral against every measured opponent, strictly more concealing by
construction, and it halves the raw suicide mass. The pruned variant trades
$2.3$ points against the heuristic for $1.6$ against gen6; against
non-equilibrium opponents, removing a $\gp$-optimal action changes matchups in
both directions. These win rates do not measure distance from equilibrium, since
none of these opponents probes the abstraction; and with guards off, self-play
logged suicide flips at $3.6\%$ of procurement decisions and hundreds of
impossible bids, so guards-on parity between variants is a floor, not a finding.

\subsection{The distance to the real game}\label{sec:distance}

Everything above is internal to $\gp$ or to a bot ladder. The real-game
instruments say the following about the solve of record.

\paragraph{The reset destroys most of the belief.} The TV meter over $2{,}000$
self-play games ($11{,}320$ boundaries) reads $\overline{\mathrm{TV}} =
\mathbf{0.842}$ $[0.839,\,0.845]$, monotone with depth: $0.66$ at the first
boundary, $0.95$ by the endgame layers. The class prior at depth is nearly
orthogonal to the belief an exact observer holds. A cross-fitted refinement
ladder localizes the loss: replacing the prior with the true class-conditional
posterior mean closes the gap only from $0.842$ to $0.671$, and enriching the
class key with freshness bits (distinguishing Paper~I's Path~A from Path~B) adds
only about $0.024$ more. Most of the destroyed belief is within-class variation
that no small public statistic reaches: the tilts of Paper~I's factorization,
which only actual belief tracking recovers.

\paragraph{The abstraction's equilibrium is qualitatively wrong in $\game$.}

\begin{theorem}[The suicide flip is $\gp$-optimal and
$\game$-dominated]\label{thm:suicide}
Call a \emph{suicide flip} a bidder's choice to flip its own deck when
its own top card is certainly off-color. In real Red/Black the action
is weakly dominated: flipping the challenger's deck preserves the
winning branch, loses the same contests, and reveals the opponent's
card instead of one's own. Yet in $\gp$, at a probe class solved with
successor values held fixed, the exact equilibrium plays the suicide
flip on path with probability $0.97$; exact best response prices it
$+0.026$ over the dominating alternative; and a solve with the action
masked cannot converge under an unmasked best response (its
exploitability floors at $0.115$).
\end{theorem}

The mechanism is the belief reset laundering the self-reveal. Busting one's own
known card raises one's public bound, and the successor re-derives beliefs from
the diffuse class prior, whereas a real opponent's posterior after a bid
followed by a deliberate bust would be devastating. The $3.6\%$ on-path suicide
rate of the raw profile is therefore not unconverged residue; it is correct play
in the wrong game. The second solve, converged two orders of magnitude tighter,
left the rate unchanged, retiring the first solve's reading of the phenomenon as
unconverged noise: convergence cannot fix an abstraction artifact at any
tolerance; only the action set or the beliefs can.

\paragraph{Certified real-game exploitability.} The LBR probe
(Definition~\ref{def:lbr}), $3{,}000$ mirrored games per profile, zero
fallbacks, certifies
\[
  \epsG \;\ge\; 0.598\ [0.569,\,0.626] \text{ per game (value range }
  [-1,1]\text{)},
\]
identically for the solve of record and for every selection variant,
against $\gp$-composed exploitability differences of $0.05$/game
between those same variants and per-round convergence of $10^{-5}$.

\begin{remark}[Head-to-head play is not a solve meter, 2026-08]
\label{rem:headtohead}
The second solve, converged $100\times$ tighter to $\gp$ than the
first, lost their raw head-to-head arena: $48.2\%$ $[46.8,\,49.6]$,
$p=0.014$, over $4{,}800$ games, a result that would have justified
reverting it. A campaign of $216{,}000$ games proved the matchup a dead
tie ($50.04\%$ $[49.8,\,50.3]$), and deterministic replay reproduced
the $48.2\%$ on its exact seed family: an unlucky draw amplified by
pair-correlated $p$-values. In $\game$ neither profile is an
equilibrium, and two non-equilibria are not ordered by their distance
to the $\gp$ solve; a gate of the form ``new solve beats old solve in
games'' tests pairwise interaction plus sampling variance, not solution
quality. The honest instruments are the composed meters and the probe.
Remark~\ref{rem:arenablind3} records a third measured instance.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The masked solve: pruning the world model}
\label{sec:maskedsolve}

Theorem~\ref{thm:suicide} makes the suicide flip the sharpest known
point of divergence between $\gp$ and $\game$, and
Remark~\ref{rem:s5refutation} shows that refusing it at play time is
worth a factor of three on the belief-carrying probe. One experiment
remained: remove the action from the solved game itself, re-solving
all of $\gp$ with the suicide flip masked out of every action set, so
that no table, no policy, and no opponent model ever contains it. The
experiment ran in 2026-08 as the store \code{solve-5-pruned}, and its
verdict is the sharpest measurement this program has of what pruning
is for.

\paragraph{The mask binds across engines.} The mask was first
verified to bind under the annealed-ladder engine of
Section~\ref{sec:colourswap}, and the verification doubles as a
cross-engine corroboration of Theorem~\ref{thm:suicide}: on the
theorem's probe class the unmasked ladder plays the flip on path at
$p=0.96$ where CFR+ played $0.97$, and masking it surrenders
$0.0497$ of that class's value where the CFR+-era measurement was
$0.0499$ (pinned by \code{tests/test\_solver\_dominance.py}). Two
engines with different update rules price the artifact identically:
the flip is genuinely $\gp$-optimal, and masking it is a real change
to the solved game.

\paragraph{The solve.} All $5{,}721$ classes were re-solved under the
fast ladder of Section~\ref{sec:stoprule} with the mask on, and the
mask was audited by read-back from the finished policies rather than
trusted from the configuration: $2{,}209$ of $2{,}209$ canonical
class-solves masked, $14{,}777{,}285$ rows locked, and the worst
probability mass on any locked row exactly $0$ (append-only
provenance in \code{solve-meta.jsonl}). The deployed resolves were
masked the same way, and both probe arms below play with guards on
and zero fallbacks, so the deployed strategy space is the masked one
end to end.

\paragraph{The verdict.} At $n=3{,}200$ games per arm on the matched
seeds of Remark~\ref{rem:s5refutation}, the belief-carrying probe
certifies
$\epsG \ge +0.1256$ $[+0.0911,\,+0.1598]$ for \code{solve-5-pruned}
against $\epsG \ge +0.0306$ $[-0.0040,\,+0.0652]$ for the guards-on
incumbent \code{solve-2-kernel}, a difference of $z=3.81$,
$p=0.00014$; the pooled artifact is
\code{pool\_solve-5-pruned\_vs\_solve2.json}. Removing the dominated
action at play time cut the probe's edge to a third; baking the same
removal into the solved tables quadrupled it.

\paragraph{Two mechanisms, decomposed.} The store a resolver carries
is also its opponent model: the belief filter conditions on the
opponent's observed actions by reading likelihoods from the same
tables. A masked model assigns probability exactly zero to an
observed suicide flip, and best responders play the flip (it is
$\gp$-attractive, Theorem~\ref{thm:suicide}), so one observed flip
breaks the filter and the game degrades to static tables for its
remainder: $806$ of $3{,}200$ verdict games broke this way, against
$0$ of $3{,}200$ in the benchmark arm. A diagnostic re-probe of the
same store with the filter's opponent transfer replaced by its
structural support indicator (Paper~III's break-proof mode, run here
as primary) eliminated the channel entirely, $0$ broken games in
$3{,}200$, and recovered about a quarter of the regression:
$\epsG \ge +0.0931$ $[+0.0585,\,+0.1275]$, still decisively worse
than the incumbent ($z=2.50$, $p=0.012$; artifact
\code{pool\_solve-5-pruned-osf\_vs\_solve2.json}).
About a quarter of the regression was therefore deployment blindness,
and the remaining three quarters is intrinsic equilibrium distortion:
the masked equilibrium prices every surrounding decision against an
action set no real opponent is restricted to, and the whole strategy
shifts with it, well beyond the removed action.

The division of labor is now measured rather than argued. Pruning a
weakly dominated action from own play is defense, and costs nothing
an opponent can use; pruning it from the model of the world is
blindness, and the store is both the play policy and that model.
Guards belong at play time, and the solved game keeps the full action
set. The store that succeeded the incumbent under exactly this
division, \code{solve-6-cfrpolicy}, which couples
\code{solve-4-qre-reach}'s values to per-class re-solved CFR+
policies, is Paper~III's subject.

\begin{remark}[Arena blindness, third instance, 2026-08]
\label{rem:arenablind3}
An arena between \code{solve-5-pruned} and \code{solve-2-kernel},
$n=5{,}000$ games per mode, read a statistical tie: $0.5078$
$[0.494,\,0.522]$, $p=0.28$ guards-off and $0.5094$, $p=0.19$
guards-on (\code{eval\_solve-5-pruned\_vs\_solve2.json}), across a
$4\times$ gap in probe-measured exploitability. This is the
third measured instance of the blindness of
Remark~\ref{rem:headtohead}, in its starkest form yet: an
arena-based verdict would have shipped the pruned store as ``no
regression'' while quadrupling real-game exploitability.
\end{remark}

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{The two axes decouple.} The central empirical finding is that
abstraction-internal quality and real-game quality are separate axes, decoupled
in both directions: profile variants differing by $0.05$/game in
$\gp$-exploitability measured identically on every real-game instrument
available at the time (the belief-carrying probe later separated the guarded
and unguarded deployments; Remark~\ref{rem:s5refutation}), and the
profile whose $\gp$ meters read $10^{-5}$ to $10^{-4}$ is exploitable for at
least $0.598$ per game in $\game$. A report citing one axis without the other
is, on this evidence, misleading regardless of which axis it picks. The
defensible claim is the conjunction Section~\ref{sec:results} reports: a stated
round-local tolerance across all classes, a composed exploitability in $\gp$,
oracle agreement where computable, mirror consistency, an explicit statement
that the profile is an equilibrium of $\gp$ rather than of Red/Black, and the
real-game instruments alongside.

\paragraph{What is established, and what is not.} Established: every class
of $\gp$ is solved reproducibly to its stated round-local tolerance, the
oracle agrees where it can see, and the distance from $\gp$ to $\game$ is
measured, with $\overline{\mathrm{TV}}=0.842$, $\epsG\ge0.598$, and one
on-path dominated action with its mechanism identified. The colour-swap
audit (Section~\ref{sec:colourswap}) bounds what "solved" delivers for
the second-generation profile: values of $\gp$ determined to about
$0.03$ and type-conditional values to about $0.37$, because CFR+'s
equilibrium selection composes as noise through the bootstrap. The
third-generation unique-selection solve restores determinacy, with its
regularization cost stated alongside. Not established: any upper bound
on $\epsG$, for this or any profile. Every real-game instrument in this paper
bounds from below.

\paragraph{The handoff.} Re-solving boundaries under the exact posterior, the
tilts this paper discards, cuts the probe's certified bound from $0.598$ to
$0.363$ at a shallow depth gate, $0.183$ deeper, and $0.078$ with full
carrying at $n=1{,}000$, a complete dose-response ($+0.0919$
$[+0.0573,\,+0.1262]$ pooled to $n=3{,}200$, and $+0.0306$
$[-0.0040,\,+0.0652]$ with play-time guards on, the deployed
configuration; Remark~\ref{rem:s5refutation}). But carrying beliefs soundly, and certifying
the result against all opponents rather than one probe family, is a different
problem with different mathematics. That problem, its theorem, its instruments,
and its current honest status are Paper~III.
