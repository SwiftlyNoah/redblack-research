\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Paper~II solved the abstraction $\gp$ of heads-up Red/Black to stated
per-class tolerances (its 2026-08 color-swap audit later bounded how
much of $\gp$ that pins down; see the remark in Section~2) and
certified that the solved profile is exploitable in the real game
$\game$ for at least $0.598$ per game of a $2.0$ range. This paper
pursues the other direction: an agent together with a computed upper
bound on its real-game exploitability. There are two results and one
honest accounting. First, carrying beliefs closes the measured gap.
Re-solving each round of play under the exact public posterior,
tracked by a filter over the factorized belief family of Paper~I and
validated to $10^{-12}$ against brute-force enumeration, produces a
dose-response in the depth of carrying: the strongest probe's lower
bound falls from $0.598$ with no carrying to $0.078\ [0.016,\,0.139]$
with full carrying, a $7.7\times$ collapse. The belief reset was most,
but demonstrably not all, of the measured exploitability: the
full-carry interval excludes zero. Second, because naive re-solving admits adversarial
counterexamples and certifies nothing, we build the machinery a
guarantee requires: a safe-resolving gadget whose per-resolve margins
are measured live by exact best-response sweeps, and a composition
theorem bounding the agent's exploitability by a root residual plus
the worst reachable chain of margins, with no opponent model anywhere
in the bound. Third, the accounting: assembling today's measured
margins yields only the trivial upper bound. An earlier assembly that
claimed $1.958$ is dissected as a cautionary result; it substituted an
on-path average for a supremum the opponent controls. The obstruction
is localized in a single quantity, the honorability of the leaf-price
surface at layers $T'\ge4$, and a four-item program targets it, each
item motivated by a measurement reported here. The sandwich read
$[0.078,\,2]$ at this paper's first close; as of 2026-08 the
belief-carrying agent over the store then served reads
$\epsG \ge -0.025\ [-0.044,\,-0.005]$ at $n=10{,}000$, a probe reading
past zero, so its sandwich is $[0,\,2]$ with the lower jaw supplied by
nonnegativity rather than by the instrument, and the upper jaw still
trivial
(the dated remarks of Section~\ref{sec:results}). The bot that players
face never re-solves. A section added in 2026-11 grades it exactly on
every class with $T'\le3$, entered at the class prior: the served
store's exploitability there reaches $0.573$, a play-time guard made it
worse until it was fixed, and an exact real-game endgame policy
certified by a verified checker to at most $6.1\times10^{-4}$
replaced the store below those classes from 2026-11-21 until it left
the serving path on 2026-11-23. On the full game the probe
bounds that bot below by $\epsG\ge1.12$, and no local certificate
moves the upper jaw off $2$. A second section added in 2026-11 gives
the first whole-game upper bound below the trivial cap, for a
non-resolving policy built to admit one: its play depends only on the
class and the seat's own type, so an opponent told that type at every
boundary has an exact dynamic program against it, and that program
bounds the policy's exploitability by $0.2998$. The bound is a
floating-point composition, reproduced by an independent
implementation, with its lowest layers verified exactly by the
checker; the probe bounds the same policy below by $0.263$
(SE $0.019$). A dated update (2026-11-23) adds split policies, which
keep the type hidden across round boundaries by private draws among
stored round policies, each solved for a range of the seat's own types;
their certificate tells the opponent only which policy was drawn. The
bot served since 2026-11-23 12:55 Eastern hides the type through two
boundaries over the vertex pack's bytes and is bounded by $0.2197$,
again in floating point, with its roots and $48$ sampled entries graded
exactly. The game is not solved: the value to the first bidder is
bracketed in $[-0.0922,\,+0.1275]$. Every number is regenerated by
scripts accompanying the papers.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Papers~I and II left heads-up Red/Black in a precisely known position.
The real game $\game$ cannot be tabulated ($2.4\times10^{33}$
infosets). Its reachable beliefs live in a rank-one family (Paper~I,
Thm.~10.3). The abstraction $\gp$, which resets beliefs at every round
boundary, is tabulable, and Paper~II solved it. Discarding the
carried beliefs costs at least $0.598$ per game of a $2.0$ payoff
range in real-game exploitability, a bound certified by Paper~II's
local best-response probe, which wins actual games against the solved
profile.

That last number is a lower bound, and no lower bound can answer the
project's remaining question:
\begin{center}
\emph{Exhibit an agent for heads-up Red/Black together with a proof
that no opponent strategy beats it by more than $\epsG$, for a small,
computed $\epsG$.}
\end{center}
A probe that fails to find an attack has measured its own reach; only
an argument that quantifies over all opponent strategies yields an
upper bound.

\paragraph{The sandwich.} Every claim in this paper lives between two
jaws: probe lower bounds (Paper~II's local best-response family,
extended here with resolver-aware variants whose opponent model is
byte-exact) and the certificate upper bound. The jaws must never
cross; a certificate below a valid probe reading refutes the
certificate rather than the probe. We report both jaws in every
results table, and the honest summary of this paper is that the lower
jaw sat at $0.078$ at its first close and has since gone past zero for
the belief-carrying agent over the store served in 2026-08
($-0.025\ [-0.044,\,-0.005]$ at
$n=10{,}000$; Remark~\ref{rem:probezero}), leaving nonnegativity as the
only lower jaw we can state, while the upper jaw sits at the trivial
cap of $2$. That is still the position of the belief-carrying agent;
the first nontrivial upper jaw in the series belongs to a different,
non-resolving policy and is reported in Section~\ref{sec:vertex}. A
second discipline governs the upper jaw: a bound that assumes the
opponent plays in any particular way, including the way our own stored
profile plays, guarantees nothing, so every certified quantity below
is either computed offline over all opponent types or measured live by
an exact best-response sweep that includes the opponent's certified
alternative.

\paragraph{The target.} The project's bar is $\epsG\le0.10$ per game
(5\% of the value range), with $0.05$ as a stretch goal. Alongside it
we require sandwich consistency, no defensive regression against the
unsafe prototype, and head-to-head and bot-battery parity under
Paper~II's standing battery.

\paragraph{Contributions.} First, a dose-response finding
(Section~\ref{sec:unsafe}): re-solving under carried beliefs drives
the strongest probe's lower bound from $0.598$ with no carrying to
$0.078\ [0.016,\,0.139]$ with full carrying, a $7.7\times$ collapse
whose interval excludes zero.
Second, safe-resolving machinery
(Sections~\ref{sec:gadget}--\ref{sec:theorem}): a gadget whose
per-resolve margins are measured live by exact best-response sweeps,
and a composition theorem bounding exploitability by a root residual
plus the worst reachable chain of margins, with no opponent model in
the bound. Third, an honest accounting (Section~\ref{sec:results}):
today's certified upper bound is the trivial one, an earlier claim of
$1.958$ is dissected as a cautionary result, and the remaining
distance is localized in the honorability of the leaf-price surface at
layers $T'\ge4$, which the four-item program of
Section~\ref{sec:program} targets. Fourth, added in 2026-11
(Section~\ref{sec:frontier-served}): an exact grading of the bot that
players face on every class with $T'\le3$, the exact real-game policy
that now replaces it there, and the verified checker that certifies
both, with the limits of what such local certificates say. Fifth,
also added in 2026-11 (Section~\ref{sec:vertex}): the first upper bound
on $\epsG$ below the trivial cap, $0.2998$ for a policy keyed on the
class and the seat's own type, with the parts verified exactly and the
parts computed in floating point separated, and a probe reading of
$0.263$ beside it.

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}

This section collects every object the rest of the paper uses.

\paragraph{The games.} $\game$ is heads-up Red/Black, a two-player
zero-sum game of hidden information; all values are seat-0 payoffs in
$[-1,1]$, so the payoff range is $U=2$. $\gp$ is the belief-reset
abstraction of $\game$ (Paper~I, Definition~10.6): the game in which,
at every round boundary, both players' beliefs are replaced by the
prior of the boundary's class, discarding the tilts accumulated during
play. Paper~II solved $\gp$ class by class; its solution (the
\emph{stored profile}, with \emph{stored values} at every class) is
the raw material for everything below.

\begin{remark}[What the stored values determine, 2026-08]
\sloppy Paper~II's color-swap audit
(\path{scripts/check_symmetry.py}) showed that the stored
second-generation profile determines the values of $\gp$ only to
about $0.03$ and its type-conditional values to about $0.37$: CFR+
pins each class's aggregate value but selects arbitrarily among
per-type-pair decompositions, and the bootstrap composes the
selection noise. Every leaf price this paper's resolves read from the
stored surface carries that indeterminacy, which is of the same order
as the per-resolve leaf dishonorability measured in
Section~\ref{sec:results}. How much of the measured leaf term is
intrinsic to depth-limited resolving and how much was the stored
surface being undetermined is answered by re-running these
instruments against the third-generation unique-selection solve
(Paper~II, Section on the color-swap audit).
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Boundaries, classes, layers.} A \emph{boundary} $b$ is a
full public history ending at a procurement resolution. Boundaries
group into the canonical \emph{classes} of Paper~I; $\kappa(b)$
denotes the class of $b$, $\lambda_\kappa$ its prior over type pairs,
and $K_b$ its deal-coupling kernel. Classes are stratified into
\emph{layers} indexed by $T'$, which decreases by one per round of
play: boundaries occur at $T'=9$ down through $T'=2$, so a game
contains at most $L_{\max}=8$ resolved boundaries (re-derived by
script).

\paragraph{Beliefs.} At a public history $h$ ending at a boundary of
class $\kappa$, the exact joint posterior over type pairs factorizes
as $\mu_h \propto \lambda_\kappa\cdot g_0\cdot g_1$, where $g_0,g_1$
are nonnegative per-seat \emph{tilt} vectors over the seats' types
(Paper~I, Thm.~10.3); the reachable belief domain is exactly this
rank-one family. The deal couples the seats only through the invariant
$a=r+x$ of each type (Paper~I, Lemma~10.2), which is what makes the
factorization survive play. $g_b$ is the agent's exact own tilt at
boundary $b$.

\paragraph{Meters.} For a strategy pair $\sigma=(\sigma_0,\sigma_1)$
in $\game$, with $u_p$ seat $p$'s expected payoff, the real-game
exploitability is
\[
\epsG(\sigma) \;:=\; \max_{\tau}\, u_1(\sigma_0,\tau)
\;+\; \max_{\tau}\, u_0(\tau,\sigma_1),
\]
the total best-response gain over both seats: zero at equilibrium,
never above $U=2$ (the trivial cap). For two distributions
$\mu,\lambda$ over type pairs,
\[
\mathrm{TV}(\mu,\lambda) \;=\; \tfrac12 \sum_{(s,t)}
\bigl|\mu(s,t)-\lambda(s,t)\bigr| ;
\]
a single reported TV number is the expectation of
$\mathrm{TV}(\mu_h,\lambda_{\kappa(h)})$ over the boundaries $h$
visited in play, weighted by reach (Paper~II's TV measurement,
2{,}000 games).

\paragraph{Probes.} A \emph{local best-response} (LBR) probe is an
opponent that, at each of its decisions, best-responds with a bounded
lookahead against the agent's actual continuation strategy, to which
it has exact access. Its realized winnings over played games certify a
statistical lower bound on $\epsG$ (mirrored seeds, Wilson 95\%
intervals): the probe wins real games, so no accounting error can
inflate the bound. A probe win rate $\mathrm{wr}$ over mirrored seats is
reported as $2(\mathrm{wr}-\tfrac12)$, the probe's mean payoff per game.
The probe's payoff in each seat is at most that seat's best-response
gain, so under the sum convention above the same games certify
$\epsG \ge 4(\mathrm{wr}-\tfrac12)$; every reported reading is
therefore a valid lower bound, conservative by a factor of $2$. This is Paper~II's probe family; the
resolver-aware variants introduced here additionally hold a
deterministic shadow copy of the resolver, checked byte-exact, so that
probing a deterministic re-solving agent uses an opponent model that
is the agent.

\paragraph{Counterfactual values.} For opponent type $t$ at a
reachable boundary $b$, $\CBV_A(b,t)$ denotes the supremum, over
opponent play below $b$, of the opponent's true conditional
counterfactual value in $\game$ against the agent's continuation,
computed under the $K_b(\cdot,t)\,g_b$ counterfactual weighting. The
composition theorem of Section~\ref{sec:theorem} is an inductive bound
on $\CBV_A$.

\section{The belief filter}\label{sec:filter}

The agent's belief tracking applies Paper~I's structure theorems
directly; its correctness burden is discharged once, here, for
everything downstream.

\paragraph{The filter.} At a boundary of class $\kappa$ the posterior
is $\mu_h \propto \lambda_\kappa\cdot g_0\cdot g_1$
(Section~\ref{sec:prelim}), and the filter tracks the two tilt vectors
by per-round transfer sums: each seat's factor is updated by the
likelihood of the public record under that seat's strategy, with the
deal coupling riding along unchanged ($a=r+x$ is invariant). Against
an independent brute-force replay of all consistent histories, the
filter reproduces the exact posterior to $10^{-12}$. The 2{,}000-game
TV measurement of Paper~II cost 64 seconds \emph{including} play, so
exact belief tracking is computationally free at this game's scale.

\paragraph{Deviation conditioning.} A re-solving agent deviates from
the stored profile, so its \emph{own} transfer must use its actually
played strategies (the filter accepts a per-seat solution override);
the opponent's transfer uses the stored profile as a model. A
shadow-replay check, run for both the unsafe and the safe resolver
(Sections~\ref{sec:unsafe} and~\ref{sec:gadget}), pins this down:
replaying a full game's public record through the filter reproduces
the resolver's tables bit for bit.

\paragraph{Conditionals that survive any opponent.} The opponent-side
transfer is a model, and a guarantee cannot lean on it. It does not
have to.

\begin{lemma}[Model-free conditionals]\label{lem:cond}
For any opponent strategy $\tau$ and public history $h$, the
conditional distribution of the agent's type given the opponent's type
is $\Pr(s\mid t,h) \propto K^{\mathrm{deal}}(a(s),a(t))\,L_A(s)$,
where $L_A$ is the agent's own likelihood sum. It does not depend on
$\tau$, and the filter update preserves it under \emph{any} strictly
positive opponent weighting, because $a$-invariance makes the deal
kernel constant along both seats' transitions.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The deal couples the seats only through $(a_0,a_1)$, deterministic
invariants of the current types. Path masses factorize per seat given
the coupling, and the opponent's factor multiplies whole columns,
cancelling from every conditional. Every own transition preserves
$a(s)$ and every opponent transition preserves $a(t)$, so the kernel
factor pulls out of both transfer sums unchanged.
\end{proof}

As a check, support-only opponent transfers reproduce the exact
filter's conditionals to $10^{-10}$ on played traces
(\code{test\_support\_filter\_conditionals}).

Two measured warnings sharpen the lemma's value. The tilts are not the
seats' standalone likelihood products: the layer prior is a different
kernel at every class, and naive per-seat products miss the kernel
drift by TV up to $0.129$ on played traces. And a zero-likelihood
opponent trace (an opponent the stored model thinks impossible) would
break a naive filter; the safe agent instead swaps the opponent
transfer for its structural support indicator and keeps resolving,
which by Lemma~\ref{lem:cond} preserves every conditional the
certificate consumes. Robustness against arbitrary opponents has to be
engineered in exactly this way. In 2026-08 the support-indicator
transfer was promoted from fallback to a primary deployment mode and
ran $3{,}200$ probed games with zero breaks and zero fallbacks
(artifacts \code{probe\_solve-5-pruned-osf\_a.json} and
\code{\_b.json}), validating it as a break-proof mode for stores
whose support is narrower than their opponents' play.

\section{Re-solving without a guarantee}\label{sec:unsafe}

The prototype changes exactly one input of Paper~II's machinery. At a
boundary with public history $h$, it solves the entry class's
one-round subgame with entry weights $\mu_h$ in place of the class
prior $\lambda_\kappa$, plays one round from the resolved strategy,
and repeats. Successor leaves keep the stored values; the kernel, the
class DAG, and the row structure are untouched.

\paragraph{Correctness checks.} A prior-weight resolve reproduces the
stored solution, so the change degenerates correctly; shadow replay
reproduces the resolver's tables byte-exactly from the public record;
full games run with zero fallbacks. Probing a deterministic resolver
requires the probe's opponent model to be the resolver itself: the
resolver-aware probe replays a deterministic shadow copy, checked to
byte-exactness, so both sides of every comparison hold equally exact
models.

\paragraph{Two findings.} First, belief carrying gains nothing
head-to-head: the resolver beats the stored profile
$50.3\%\ [47.2,53.4]$. An equilibrium of the correctly-believed
subgame does not harvest a fixed opponent; only best response does.
Second, carrying is a large \emph{defensive} win
(Figure~\ref{fig:dose}). The cost anti-correlates with the benefit:
the deep layers where TV is worst ($0.92$--$0.95$) are precisely the
cheapest to resolve ($\le1$\,s), which is what makes full carrying
practical.

\paragraph{Why this certifies nothing.} Three obstructions, each
fatal alone.
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item Unsafe re-solving has no theorem. Each resolve derives $\mu_h$
      assuming the agent's own past strategy; an opponent who knows
      this can deviate early to poison the assumption and cash in
      later. Known constructions in the re-solving literature make
      naive re-solving strictly \emph{more} exploitable. Our probe
      found no such attack, which says more about the probe than about
      the agent.
\item The filter's opponent transfer is a model
      (Section~\ref{sec:filter}); a guarantee must hold against
      opponents no filter models.
\item The leaves are $\gp$-flavored. Every resolve prices its exits
      with stored values computed under class priors at deeper
      boundaries; their error \emph{as $\game$-values} is the dominant
      unknown, and Section~\ref{sec:frontier} measures it as
      substantial ($0.146$ worst-case at $T'{=}3$).
\end{enumerate}
The structural inequality
$\epsG \le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{comp}}
+ 2U\cdot\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{TV}]\cdot\mathrm{depth}$,
with $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{comp}}$ the profile's composed
within-abstraction exploitability and depth the number of carried
boundaries, is vacuous at the measured $\mathrm{TV}=0.842$ (right side
${\approx}10$ against a trivial cap of $2$) and does not touch
obstruction~1 at all. A certificate therefore requires a different
agent, the one the theorem of Section~\ref{sec:theorem} is about.

\section{Safe re-solving}\label{sec:gadget}

Fix the agent seat $p$ and opponent $q$. The agent plays the stored
root solve at the game root and, at every boundary it reaches, the
strategy extracted from the gadget solve below. The \emph{leaf
functional} $\hat v_b(b',s',t')\in[-1,1]$ prices round-end leaves
(currently the stored values; Section~\ref{sec:frontier} upgrades
them), and terminal leaves are exact.

\begin{definition}[Safe-resolving gadget]\label{def:gadget}
At boundary $b$ with own range $g_b$ and any strictly positive
weighting $\nu_b$ on the opponent's types, the gadget game
$\tilde G(b)$ is: chance draws $(s,t)$ with weight
$K_b(s,t)\,g_b(s)\,\nu_b(t)$; the opponent observes $t$ and chooses
\textsc{out}, ending the game at the \emph{promise} $w_b(s,t)$, or
\textsc{in}, after which the one-round subgame of $\kappa(b)$ is
played with leaves priced by $\hat v_b$. The agent never opts out.
\end{definition}

Define the conditional promise
$\hat w_b(t) := \sum_s K_b(s,t)\,g_b(s)\,w_b(s,t) \,/\, M_b(t)$ with
$M_b(t) := \sum_s K_b(s,t)\,g_b(s)$, and for any agent round strategy
$\sigma$ and opponent behavior $\tau$ the opponent's conditional
counterfactual value $\tilde V^{\tau}_q(b,t;\sigma)$ in the gadget.
The weighting $\nu_b$ cancels from every conditional quantity; it
tunes convergence only.

\begin{lemma}[Margin identity]\label{lem:margin}
Let $\sigma_b$ be the agent's average strategy from the gadget solve
and $m_b(t) := \max_{\tau} \tilde V^{\tau}_q(b,t;\sigma_b) - \hat w_b(t)$
its measured per-type margin (one exact best-response sweep, recorded
live). Then $m_b(t)\ge0$, and every opponent behavior in the gadget
satisfies $\tilde V^{\tau}_q(b,t;\sigma_b) \le \hat w_b(t)+m_b(t)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The best response includes \textsc{out}, whose conditional value is
exactly $\hat w_b(t)$, so the maximum is at least $\hat w_b(t)$; the
inequality is the definition of the maximum. The sweep is exact: the
opponent's in-round information sets are (public node, private state)
pairs, swept by per-state argmax against path-summed agent reach, then
a per-type maximum over pre-stage choices including \textsc{out}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Carry-down consistency]\label{lem:carry}
Construct promises and prices from \emph{one} functional $\hat v$: at
every resolve, $w_b := \hat v$ on the entry grid of $\kappa(b)$, and
leaves priced by $\hat v_b$. If $b'$ is a successor boundary reached
from the resolve at $b$, with $g_{b'}$ the played-strategy transfer of
$g_b$, then the conditional promise of the next resolve equals the
conditional leaf price the $b$-resolve charged:
$\hat w_{b'}(t') = \sum_{s'} K_{b'}g_{b'}\hat v_b(b',s',t')/M_{b'}(t')$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Both sides are the same $g_{b'}$-weighted average of the same matrix:
the filter propagates its own factor under exactly the played strategy
(the byte-exact shadow-replay check), and the opponent factor cancels
from the conditional by Lemma~\ref{lem:cond}. Verified as a
float-precision identity on played traces.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[One-sidedness, and why it matters]\label{rem:oneside}
Lemma~\ref{lem:carry} is an equality for the one-functional
construction, but the telescoping argument below survives promises
\emph{at or below} the parent's leaf conditionals: a promise may be
tightened downward without breaking soundness. This freedom is what
the ``hybrid'' promise experiments of Section~\ref{sec:results}
price, and their measured failure mode (tightening without enforcement
is harmful) is one of the sharpest lessons of that section.
\end{remark}

In implementation, a prior-weight gadget solve reproduces the stored
value (margins under $20\times$ solver tolerance), the compiled kernel
and the reference implementation agree on the gadget game to $10^{-9}$
at identical iteration counts, safe shadow replay is byte-exact
($10^{-12}$), and full games run with zero fallbacks, including forced
filter-break survival. The gadget's conservatism costs $1.4$
percentage points head-to-head against the unsafe resolver
(confidence intervals overlapping) at
$\varepsilon_{\max}=8.9\times10^{-4}$ per resolve.

\section{The composition theorem}\label{sec:theorem}

\begin{assumption}\label{ass:full}
Full carry: every non-terminal boundary the agent reaches is resolved.
A depth-limited agent adds, per unresolved round, an uncertified
belief-reset term; the certificate is a full-carry object.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}\label{ass:break}
No unrepaired filter break. Safe mode enforces this by construction
(Section~\ref{sec:filter}); the repair preserves every conditional the
certificate consumes and cannot itself break.
\end{assumption}

Reachable chains are sequences $(b_1,t_1)\to\cdots\to(b_L,t_L)$ of
successor boundaries and opponent types under the agent's public
dynamics; by Section~\ref{sec:prelim} a game has at most
$L_{\max}=8$ resolved boundaries ($T'=9,\dots,2$).

\begin{theorem}[Composition, measured form]\label{thm:comp}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:full} and~\ref{ass:break}, for every
opponent strategy $\tau$:
\[
u_q(A,\tau) \;\le\; B^{\mathrm{root}}_q \;+\;
\pathmax{(b_1,t_1)\to\cdots\to(b_L,t_L)}\;\sum_{i=1}^{L} m_{b_i}(t_i),
\]
where $B^{\mathrm{root}}_q$ is the opponent's exact best-response value
against the stored root tables in the $\hat v$-priced root round, and
the maximum ranges over reachable chains. For the mirrored agent pair,
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pair}} \le
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{round}}(\mathrm{root}) +
\sum_{\mathrm{seats}}\pathmax{\mathrm{chains}}\sum_i m_{b_i}(t_i)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Bottom-up induction on boundaries with
$D(b,t) := m_b(t) + \max_{(b',t')\text{ reachable}} D(b',t')$ (empty
max $=0$); the claim is $\CBV_A(b,t)\le\hat w_b(t)+D(b,t)$.

\emph{Base}: a resolve whose leaves are all terminal has $\hat v$
exact, so the gadget \emph{is} the truth below $b$ restricted to
\textsc{in}, and Lemma~\ref{lem:margin} closes it.

\emph{Step}: fix $\tau$ below $b$. The true conditional value
decomposes over the round's leaf aggregates: writing $\rho_\tau$ for
opponent-and-chance reach into the aggregate landing on $(b',t')$,
\[
V^{\tau}_q(b,t) = \tilde V^{\tau}_q(b,t;\sigma_b)
+ \sum_{(b',t')} \rho_\tau\Bigl[\,V^{\tau}_q(b',t')
- \tfrac{\sum_{s'}K_{b'}g_{b'}\hat v_b(b',s',t')}{M_{b'}(t')}\Bigr],
\]
because gadget and truth differ only in leaf pricing, and the agent's
reach into each leaf under $\sigma_b$ is exactly what defines
$g_{b'}$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:carry} the subtracted term is
$\hat w_{b'}(t')$; by induction the bracket is at most $D(b',t')$;
by Lemma~\ref{lem:margin} the first term is at most
$\hat w_b(t)+m_b(t)$. Take suprema.

\emph{Root}: the same decomposition applied once to the stored root
round, prior-weighted over root types, plus the zero-sum meter
identity $B^{\mathrm{root}}_0+B^{\mathrm{root}}_1 =
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{round}}(\mathrm{root})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The bound is opponent-model-free: the weighting $\nu_b$ and the
filtered opponent factor influence which equilibrium the resolve
finds, hence how small the margins come out, but never the validity of
the theorem. This is exactly the property unsafe re-solving lacks.
\end{remark}

End-to-end verification: the composed two-level agent (a gadget
resolve at a $T'{=}3$ boundary plus per-history gadget resolves at its
$T'{=}2$ successors) is transcribed into the flattened perfect-recall
oracle and the opponent is exact-best-responded per type; the result
matches $\hat w$ plus the chain-sum of recorded margins in 5/5
configurations (both seats, prior and tilted entries, including the
worst promise-dishonor class), and carry-down consistency holds to
$3\times10^{-16}$.

\section{Value-of-belief functions and the exact frontier}
\label{sec:frontier}

The theorem charges margins, and margins are small only where promises
are \emph{honorable} (backed by something true in $\game$). The
stored values are not: at $T'{=}3$, $37$ of $66$ class values sit
outside their exact-in-$\game$ brackets, worst case $0.146$ (stored
$-0.657$ against exact $-0.510\ [-0.5105,-0.5100]$), reach-weighted
$1.36\times10^{-2}$. This is the within-subgame belief-reset
composition error, and it agrees with the full-carry probe residual
($0.078$), a cross-instrument consistency. The certificate
therefore needs a new object: exact values of class subgames \emph{as
functions of entry belief}.

\paragraph{The domain correction.} $V(\kappa,\mu)$ is not a function
on the full belief simplex and is not piecewise-linear there. By
Paper~I's factorization (Thm.~10.3) the reachable domain is the
rank-one family, and restricted to it $V$ is
piecewise-\emph{bilinear} in the tilt pair $(g_0,g_1)$: concave
piecewise-linear in each tilt with the other fixed. A facet census
confirmed the model: across sampled seat segments the worst
midpoint-above-chord excess is negative (every midpoint at least
$1.4\times10^{-3}$ below chord plus certified slack).

\paragraph{The facet census.} The operational complexity measure is
the size of a greedy \emph{strategy-library cover}: solved round
strategies such that exact best-response sandwiches (lower and upper
envelopes over library entries) close to $10^{-3}$ over a set of
candidate beliefs. Full population at $T'\le3$:

vertices (holdout max $0.657$), a weakness that returns with force in
Section~\ref{sec:results}. At $T'{=}4$, buildability and reach
\emph{anti-correlate}: the most-visited classes exceed flat-tree build
budgets, so deep-layer certification leans on functional pricing at
$T'{=}5$ boundaries rather than exact $T'{=}4$ leaves for the heavy
classes. Frontier validation is oracle-, mirror- and
$T'{=}2$-containment-based; by the measurement above, stored-value
equality is not required above $T'{=}2$.

\paragraph{What the frontier owes the certificate.} The theorem
quantifies over all opponents, including ones steering to boundaries
no probe visits, so the certificate needs an a-priori bound on
$m_b(t)$ valid at every reachable $(b,g_b)$. For each covered class
$\kappa$ and every member $g$ of its factored entry family, three
objects are required: an \emph{honorable promise vector}
$w_\kappa(g,\cdot)$, a certified guarantee that some playable library
strategy caps the opponent's true conditional counterfactual value
below the boundary at $w_\kappa(g,t)+r_\kappa(g,t)$ for all $t$
simultaneously, with certified residual $r$; the \emph{functional
sandwich gap} $\Delta_\kappa(g,t)$ between the library's envelopes
evaluated at the resolve's own entry ranges (two exact best-response
sweeps per entry, ${\sim}5$\,ms), the leaf-error norm of record; and
an \emph{off-path enforcement rule}, under which the resolver compares
its solve's measured margins against the capping entry's and plays the
better, converting measured margins into the a-priori form
$m_b(t)\le\varepsilon_b+r_{\kappa(b)}(g_b,t)$.

\begin{corollary}[Certified form]\label{cor:cert}
With all three objects in place,
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pair}} \;\le\;
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{round}}(\mathrm{root}) +
\sum_{\mathrm{seats}}\pathmax{\mathrm{chains}}
\sum_{i=1}^{L\le L_{\max}}
[\,\varepsilon_{b_i} + r_{\kappa(b_i)}(g_{b_i},t_i)\,],
\]
every term computed offline or recorded live.
\end{corollary}

The $0.11$--$0.26$ per-resolve charge of Table~\ref{tab:layerterms}

\begin{remark}[A rejected certificate]\label{rem:rejected}
Summing Table~\ref{tab:layerterms}'s on-path terms over $L_{\max}=8$
and both seats gives $1.958$, and an earlier draft of this section
(dated 2026-08-03 in the project records) reported that number as the
certificate. An independent adversarial audit rejected it: the
theorem's chain maximum ranges over \emph{opponent-steerable} chains,
and an on-path mean substitutes a self-play average for a supremum the
opponent controls. The licensed assembly from the same data, the worst
observed chain over visited boundaries only, is $8.22$: vacuous.
Reach-weighting is not a sound repair, because the opponent steers the
carried beliefs and self-play samples a measure-zero slice of the
chain space. The audit's verdict, adopted in full: certified $\epsG$
today is the trivial cap. The sound averaging opportunities that
survive (root-prior weighting over the opponent's root type, and a
chance-expectation reproof of the successor step, whose
$0.4$--$2.0$ verification slack locates the loose inequality) are
theorem work, listed in the program. The lesson: in certification, the
assembly is as much a proof obligation as the per-piece inequalities,
and an average is never a supremum.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Fixed promise surfaces cannot certify.} The audit also
produced the program's most decisive positive tool. Per-type dishonor
of a \emph{fixed} promise surface is linear-fractional in the own
tilt, so its supremum over the rank-one family is attained at own-type
vertices and can be certified exactly (a clairvoyant best response per
vertex). The functional surface's certified supremum dishonor is
median ${\approx}1.0$ and max $2.0$ at \emph{both} $T'{=}2$ and
$T'{=}3$, even where on-path margins measure $3\times10^{-4}$. An earlier holdout measurement suggesting fixed
refined surfaces might suffice (max $0.063$) was a sampling artifact
of insufficiently extreme tilts. Any certificate path through a fixed
promise matrix is closed, permanently, at every layer. What remains
open is \emph{range-dependent} promises (library caps evaluated at
the realized entry range) with per-type enforcement, whose residual is
the library sandwich-gap supremum, itself lower-bounded by vertex
probes at median $0.14$--$0.43$ today; hence the vertex-completion
item in the program.

\paragraph{Enforcement is mandatory.} Honorable (tighter) promises
push opponent opt-out mass up, and CFR's weighted objective then
simply ignores opted-out types, whose margins are only weakly
controlled by the solve. The measurement: hybrid promises (tightened
by Remark~\ref{rem:oneside}) \emph{worsen} the $T'{=}3$ margin term
from $0.085$ to $0.634$ as built, because the meter measures against
the tighter bar while the solve stops defending it. The audit's
a-priori judgment (``sound but worthless without enforcement'') was
generous; the measurement says actively harmful. Post-solve per-type
minimization with the capping entry (the enforcement rule of
Section~\ref{sec:frontier}) is load-bearing machinery.

\begin{remark}[Range-dependent leaf prices, and the fixed point they
create, 2026-08]\label{rem:fixedpoint}
Since a fixed promise surface cannot certify, the leaf price must be
evaluated at the successor's realized entry range. That range is a
function of the agent's own play and the public record alone, never of
the opponent's private choices, so it is admissible in a bound. Two
requirements emerged in building it. The price must see the full public
resolution, not merely the successor class: the bid chain and the
location of the fatal flip both move the range while leaving the class
alone. And the price must be a per-type CONSTANT rather than a per-pair
matrix, because a parent contracts a leaf price against its own reaches,
which are not the weights the cap was computed under; a first
implementation with per-pair caps measured a \emph{negative} excess
($-5.4\times10^{-3}$), which is to say it did not cap. Per-type
constants restored it ($+8.2\times10^{-5}$ and $+8.5\times10^{-5}$ in
the two directions), which is the promise vector $\hat w$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:margin} arrived at from the other end.

With that in place the per-resolve residual factors exactly as
\[
  r = \varepsilon_b + (\text{successor sandwich at its realized range}),
\]
verified to $10^{-5}$. Measured at the class prior, with successors
solved exactly at their realized ranges wherever a perfect-recall tree
builds, the sandwich is $3.0\times10^{-4}$ to $4.5\times10^{-4}$ at
$T'{=}3$ and $3.1\times10^{-4}$ at $T'{=}4$, against the
$6.3\times10^{-3}$ per resolve Corollary~\ref{cor:cert} allows. Two
rungs of the ladder are therefore flat and far inside the budget.

The extrapolation stops there, and an earlier draft of this remark did
not. Exact successor solves exist only where a tree builds, which is
$T'\le3$ entirely and $T'{=}4$ for $80$ of $165$ classes covering $34\%$
of played mass. At $T'{=}5$ the successors must be priced from stored
libraries, and the sandwich is $1.1\times10^{-2}$, thirty-five times the
layers below. That figure confounds two causes: the ladder fattening, and
the libraries being thin. It leans towards the latter, since enriching
the library from four entries to all of them moves it from
$3.2\times10^{-2}$ to $1.1\times10^{-2}$, and since the hard $T'{=}4$
covers were themselves left open at residual $3.6\times10^{-2}$ to
$1.5\times10^{-1}$ by an entry cap imposed for time. Layers $6$ through
$9$, which carry most of the charge, remain untouched.

What binds is a fixed point: the price depends on the successor's range,
the range on the parent's play, the play on the price. Solving afresh at
each repricing oscillates ($r$ swinging between $0.32$ and $1.19$).
Warm-started repricing, damping, and dropping the running average each
settle three of six measured classes at $1.5$--$2.2\times10^{-4}$ and
leave the other three unsettled; freezing the successor strategy stops
the price moving but inflates the successor sandwich to $1.9$, since a
strategy committed at one range is worthless at another. Two natural
culprits are ruled out by measurement: independently produced
equilibria of the same successor at the same range move the per-type
caps by at most $3.3\times10^{-3}$, and no leaf range is degenerate.
Depth-limited solvers whose leaf values depend on the ranges the solve
produces re-evaluate those values at the current iterate on every
iteration; that variant is untried here.

A structural consequence outranks it. Pricing a leaf at $T'{=}6$ by
recursion would require the $T'{=}5$ cap at its realized range, which
requires a round sweep over $T'{=}4$ leaves priced at theirs, and so on;
with $5.0\times10^{4}$ public resolutions in a $T'{=}9$ round falling to
$32$ at $T'{=}2$, the product is astronomical. The certificate therefore
needs a per-class, range-parameterized cap \emph{function} at every
layer, built once from the bottom and stored, so that pricing a leaf is
a lookup. Storing the values is cheap, a few megabytes over all $2{,}860$
classes. Storing the strategies the enforcement rule must commit to is
not: a round strategy at $T'{=}9$ carries $2.2\times10^{5}$ infoset rows.
Constructing and pricing that ladder is the open problem, and the
fixed point above governs how expensive each rung is rather than whether
it exists.
\end{remark}

\section{The program}\label{sec:program}

Four items, each with the measurement that motivates it, in
dependency order. The research risk is concentrated in item~2.

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep3pt
\item \textbf{Vertex-complete frontier libraries at $T'\le4$.} One
      exact solve per feasible own-type vertex ($10$--$144$ per class,
      sub-second each) collapses the library gap at vertices to solve
      tolerance; the interior supremum then follows from the
      piecewise-bilinear structure by small bilinear programs.
      Target: certified per-class residual $r_\kappa$ at $T'\le4$ of
      order $5\times10^{-3}$. The vertex probes of
      Section~\ref{sec:results} say the gap is real and say exactly
      where.
\item \textbf{Certified cap propagation upward.} A $T'{\ge}5$ cap
      against a strategy is one kernel exact-best-response round sweep
      with capped leaves, sound by Theorem~\ref{thm:comp}'s machinery.
      The open question is whether six layers of propagation stay
      tight. Remark~\ref{rem:fixedpoint} reports two rungs flat and far
      inside budget, a third that is thirty-five times worse for
      reasons not yet separated, and the rest untouched. This remains
      the program's research risk, now with a cost model attached.
\item \textbf{Enforcement transcription.} Library entries transcribed
      to playable round strategies, with per-type minimization at
      resolve time. This converts measured margins into a-priori
      bounds and flips the hybrid experiment from harmful to a strict
      win.
\item \textbf{Sound accounting.} The averaging opportunities that
      survive the audit: margin de-duplication (measured $-0.007$),
      $a$-invariant chain assembly (per-type margin vectors are
      persisted for this), root-prior weighting, and the
      chance-expectation reproof of the successor step.
\end{enumerate}

Probe power becomes worth buying only after items 1--3 move the
certificate; the next result lives in the upper jaw. Serving latency,
multiplayer, public-statistic enrichment (ruled out by Paper~II's
refinement ladder), and further equilibrium-set selection (real-game
noise, by Paper~II's measurements) are out of scope for this program.

\begin{remark}[Paused, with a lower bound only, 2026-08; amended
2026-11]\label{rem:paused}
Two measurements taken after this program paused settle how it stands.
First, the hypothesis that the leaf-price dishonorability of
Section~\ref{sec:results} was largely the incumbent store's color
inconsistency in disguise was refuted: against a re-solve whose values
are color-exact to $3.5\times10^{-9}$ where the incumbent is off by
$1.67$ (Paper~II's third-generation solve), the resolver-aware probe's
edge did not fall ($0.122\ [0.060,0.183]$ against the incumbent's
$0.078\ [0.016,0.139]$, $p=0.32$, two-proportion test at $n=1000$ per
arm; \code{probe\_resolver.py}, artifacts \code{probe\_v3.json} and
\code{probe\_v10.json}). The dishonorability is therefore intrinsic to
pricing depth-limited resolves from a stored belief-reset surface, and
a better store cannot remove it. Second, the exact frontier surface
that would replace those prices is infeasible above $T'\approx4$
(Section~\ref{sec:results}). The statement of record follows: heads-up
Red/Black ships with the measured lower bound
$\epsG\ge0.078\ [0.016,0.139]$ under full belief carrying ($\ge0.598$
for the stored profile) and with no certified upper bound below the
trivial cap of $2$. This position is recorded deliberately, so that no
reader infers an upper bound from the machinery's existence. The

\section{The served bot, graded exactly at the frontier, 2026-11}

\paragraph{The subgame and its meter.} For a class $C$, let
$\Gamma(C)$ be the game in which chance deals both seats' private types
from the class prior $\lambda_C$, after which the real rules of $\game$
are played from $C$'s boundary until a seat is eliminated, by players
with perfect recall who see the order of every flip. For a profile
$\sigma$ of $\Gamma(C)$,
\[
\varepsilon_{\Gamma(C)}(\sigma) \;:=\; \max_{\tau}\, u_0(\tau,\sigma_1)
\;+\; \max_{\tau}\, u_1(\sigma_0,\tau),
\]
the same sum over both seats as $\epsG$ in Section~\ref{sec:prelim},
with cap $2$. The companion computations (the builder, the exact best
response and the verified checker of the repository
\code{call-the-bluff}, and the book certificates of the game repository)
report half this sum, \texttt{nash\_conv}$/2$. Every figure from them
in this section is that output doubled, so all exploitabilities below
are in the units of $\epsG$. The solver's own round-local meter
$\varepsilon_{\text{round}}$ is already a sum and is quoted as
computed. There are $36$ classes at $T'=2$ and $132$ at $T'=3$; their
trees have $641$ to $5{,}081$ nodes at $T'=2$ and $9{,}136$ to
$223{,}697$ (median $55{,}929$) at $T'=3$.

\emph{noprune} guards, served from 2026-11-21 to 2026-11-23, keep only the forced
challenge of a standing bid that is provably false and the suicide-flip

\paragraph{Brittleness of the vertex build, and why the CFR+ build is
served.} Both certificates hold at the class prior, and real play
enters $C$ with a posterior from the factorized family
$\lambda_C\cdot g_0\cdot g_1$. Graded at tilted entry beliefs (per-type
factors $g=\exp(N(0,0.5^2))$, $20$ tilts per class, the same tilts for
every profile, float exact best response;
\path{redblack/rb-track-out/gbook/tilted_vs_served.py} in
\code{call-the-bluff}), the median over the $132$ classes at $T'=3$ of
each class's median exploitability is $0.220$ for the raw pack, $0.289$
with the old guards, $0.160$ for \code{gbook} and $0.087$ for
\code{gbook-cfr}; the medians of the per-class worst tilt are $0.437$,
$0.550$, $0.368$ and $0.248$. \code{gbook-cfr} is the less exploitable
book at tilted beliefs on $86$ of the $132$ classes and beats the raw
pack on $83$. A linear program returns a vertex of the equilibrium
polytope, an extreme equilibrium whose opponent indifferences are
balanced at exactly $\lambda_C$; when the belief moves, those balances
break and the vertex's sharp rows are what an exploiter farms. The

degrades more slowly. This is an interpretation consistent with the
measurement, not a theorem. \code{gbook-cfr} is therefore the build

about $600$ in the certificate at the prior and is the more robust

at a posterior, which would need a resolve at play time.

\section{An upper bound below the trivial cap: the vertex ladder,
2026-11}\label{sec:vertex}

Every upper bound in the sections above is the trivial $2$. This
section reports the first below it. It belongs to a non-resolving
policy built so that a bound is cheap to state, called the
\emph{vertex policy} below, and it comes from a told-type argument
rather than from the composition theorem of
Section~\ref{sec:theorem}, so it says nothing about the
belief-carrying agent. Its parts are of two kinds, verified exactly
by the checker of Section~\ref{sec:frontier-served} and computed in
floating point, and the section keeps them apart. Every number is
printed by \path{simulator/scripts/paper3_vertex_numbers.py} from the
dated folder \path{results/solver/paper3-vertex/}
(Appendix~\ref{sec:verification}).

\paragraph{The policy.} A seat's \emph{type} at a boundary is the pair
$s=(r,x)$ of Paper~I, its red cards in hand and among its own
discards. For each class $C$ with $T'\le9$ and each type $s$ the agent
seat can hold there, one CFR+ solve plays the round of $C$ with the
agent's type fixed at $s$ and revealed to the opponent, and prices each
procurement resolution that does not end the game by the successor
class's matrix $L$ defined next. The solve runs to a round bracket of
$2\times10^{-3}$ or $600$ iterations. One exact best-response sweep
over the solved round then gives
\[
L(C,s,t) \;=\; \min_{\tau}\; \text{(agent's value in the round of $C$, agent type $s$, opponent type $t$)},
\]
the minimum over opponent strategies $\tau$ that see $s$, with leaves
priced by the successor's $L$. Layers run bottom up from $T'=2$, so a
successor's matrix always exists: $5{,}720$ classes and $25{,}222$
(class, type) solves in all, $3{,}376$~s on $8$ workers. The opening
round reveals nothing and is solved once per agent seat over the whole
deal prior, with leaves at the $T'=9$ matrices. Agent seat $1$ plays in
class $C$ the rows solved for seat $0$ in the class with the seats
swapped. The policy, written $\sigma^{V}$, is the resulting profile:
in every round its play is a function of the class, the seat's type at
the class's boundary, and what the seat has seen within the round.

\begin{lemma}[Told-type bound]\label{lem:toldtype}
Let $v_{\mathrm{lo}}$ be the value seat $0$ secures with
$\sigma^{V}_0$ in the opening round against an opponent who best
responds there with leaves priced by $L$, and $v_{\mathrm{hi}}$ the
largest value seat $0$ obtains against $\sigma^{V}_1$ in the opening
round with leaves so priced. Then
$u_0(\sigma^{V}_0,\tau)\ge v_{\mathrm{lo}}$ and
$u_0(\tau,\sigma^{V}_1)\le v_{\mathrm{hi}}$ for every strategy $\tau$
of $\game$, so
$\epsG(\sigma^{V})\le v_{\mathrm{hi}}-v_{\mathrm{lo}}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Fix a boundary of class $C$ at which the agent has type $s$ and the
opponent type $t$. An opponent in $\game$ decides from the public
history and its own cards; an opponent told $s$ knows at least that
at every later decision of the round. The agent's next discard and
order are drawn afresh given $(C,s)$, so the history before the
boundary changes nothing about the agent's play after it. Any
continuation of a $\game$ strategy below the boundary is therefore a
strategy the told-type opponent may play, and by induction on $T'$ it
leaves the agent at least $L(C,s,t)$. At the opening round nothing is
told, and $v_{\mathrm{lo}}$ is the exact best response there against
leaves that are lower bounds; $v_{\mathrm{hi}}$ is symmetric.
\end{proof}

The bound holds for any rows, converged or not: the solves' brackets
(median over classes of the worst type $1.8\times10^{-3}$, largest
$6.5\times10^{-3}$) affect only how tight it is.

\paragraph{The bound.} The ladder gives
$v_{\mathrm{lo}}=-0.12470$ and $v_{\mathrm{hi}}=+0.17506$, so
\[
\epsG(\sigma^{V}) \;\le\; 0.2998,
\]
in the sum units of Section~\ref{sec:prelim}; the companion checker's
\texttt{nash\_conv}$/2$ would read $0.1499$. Seat $0$ of the vertex
policy loses at most $0.1247$ per game to any opponent, and seat $1$
concedes at most $0.1751$.

\paragraph{What is verified, and what is floating point.}
Table~\ref{tab:vertexchecks} lists four checks, each run on the float
rows the ladder saved, the last three without the ladder's
best-response code, and the second without any code from the game

\paragraph{Why the vertex bound is loose.} Lemma~\ref{lem:toldtype}
prices a policy against an opponent told the agent's type at every
boundary, and $\sigma^{V}$ was solved against exactly that opponent, so
it has no reason to hide its type and does not: the probe takes $0.263$
of its $0.2998$. A tighter bound therefore needs a policy that tells
less, and a certificate whose opponent is told only what the policy
lets slip.

\paragraph{Split policies.} Fix the agent seat $A$. At a boundary into
class $C$, write $K_C(s,t)$ for the prior of the two seats' types
(hypergeometric, Paper~I). An \emph{entry} of $C$ is a triple
$e=(g_e,\pi_e,Q_e)$: a \emph{design range} $g_e$, a weight in $[0,1]$
on each type $s$ of $A$, read relative to $K_C$; a round policy
$\pi_e(\cdot\mid s)$ for every $s$ in the support of $g_e$; and for
every way $p$ the round can end in a successor class $C'$ a \emph{draw
table} $Q_e(\cdot\mid p,s')$, a distribution over the entries of $C'$
and one absorbing symbol $\mathsf{V}$, indexed by $A$'s type $s'$ at
that boundary. A \emph{split policy} $\sigma^{S}$ is a library of
entries per class and seat together with one root entry per seat. The
bot that holds $e$ with type $s$ plays $\pi_e(\cdot\mid s)$ through the
round; when the round ends at $p$ it draws $j\sim Q_e(\cdot\mid p,s')$
with private coins and holds $j$ through the next round, and after
$\mathsf{V}$ it plays the vertex pack's rows for the rest of the game.
Each round policy is solved by CFR+ for a whole range of the bot's own
types, so holding an entry says which range, not which hand. Stored
entries are fixed bytes: rows quantized as the pack's are, pre-stage
frequencies to six decimals, draw weights as integers over $2^{16}$,
and every quantity below is computed on those bytes.

\paragraph{What the opponent in the certificate is told.} At every
boundary it is told the index $j$ of the entry the bot draws, and after
a draw of $\mathsf{V}$ it is told the type $s'$, as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:toldtype}. It is never told the type while the bot holds
a stored entry. A real opponent is told nothing, so each of its
strategies is a strategy of the told one that ignores what it is told,
and a guarantee against the told opponent holds against it.

\paragraph{The certificate of an entry.} An entry $e$ of class $C$
carries a vector $U_e$ with the guarantee that, for every opponent
strategy and every opponent type $t$,
\[
\sum_s K_C(s,t)\,g_e(s)\;\mathbb{E}\big[\text{$A$'s payoff}\mid s,t\big]\;\ge\;U_e(t)
\]
when $A$'s range is $g_e$ and $A$ plays $e$ as above. Three facts give
it, bottom up. (i) \emph{The boundary joint factorizes.} Discards
leave each seat's dealt reds unchanged, and $K$ couples the two seats
only through them, so at a round end $p$ into $C'$ the joint mass of
the next types is $K_{C'}(s',t')\,g_p(s')\,f_p(t')$ with $g_p$ a
function of $A$'s play alone; whatever the opponent did, its posterior
over $s'$ is the $K_{C'}$-conditional of $g_p$. (ii) \emph{Capped draws
with charged deficits.} The mass that plays entry $j$ is
$h_j(s')=g_p(s')\,Q_e(j\mid p,s')$. With the cap
$c_j=\max_{s'}h_j(s')/g_j(s')$, so that $h_j\le c_j\,g_j$, entry $j$
secures against opponent type $t'$ at least
\[
c_j\,U_j(t')\;-\;\sum_{s'}\big(c_j\,g_j(s')-h_j(s')\big)\,K_{C'}(s',t'),
\]
the deficit $c_jg_j-h_j$ being mass that $U_j$ counts and the bot does
not hold there, charged at the largest payoff $+1$; it never secures
less than $-\sum_{s'}h_j(s')K_{C'}(s',t')$. Mass drawn to $\mathsf{V}$
with type $s'$ secures $K_{C'}(s',t')\,L(C',s',t')$, with $L$ the
told-type matrix of the vertex ladder recomputed for the served pack's
bytes. Each round end is priced by the sum of these terms over the
draws. (iii) \emph{One exact best response per entry.} With the round
ends so priced, one exact best-response sweep of $C$'s round by the
opponent, per opponent private state with perfect recall inside the
round, gives $U_e$. The bot's procurement rows are restricted to the
canonical flip order (the challenger's cards first, then its own), so
the public order of flips is a function of the merged state key and
shows the opponent nothing the sweep does not already see.

\begin{lemma}[Told-entry bound]\label{lem:toldentry}
Let $v_{\mathrm{lo}}=\sum_t U_{\rho_0}(t)$ for the root entry $\rho_0$
of agent seat $0$, and let $v_{\mathrm{hi}}$ be the matching quantity
for seat $1$ in seat-$0$ units. Then
$\epsG(\sigma^{S})\le v_{\mathrm{hi}}-v_{\mathrm{lo}}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By induction on $T'$, as for Lemma~\ref{lem:toldtype} with the drawn
entry in place of the type. At a boundary the bot's next play depends
on the history only through the drawn entry and its own type, and by
(i) the told opponent's posterior over that type, given the entry, is
the one the entry's range and the draw induce. So the continuation of
any strategy of $\game$ is a strategy of the told opponent below the
boundary, and by (ii) and the induction hypothesis it leaves the agent
at least the price of that round end. Step (iii) is the exact best
response over the round with those prices. The draws of a library may
send mass to several entries at once and the caps make each entry's
guarantee hold for the mass it actually receives.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Composition over two boundaries.} In store \code{r1} the
root's draws at the first boundary go to entries at $T'=9$ whose draws
all go to $\mathsf{V}$. In store \code{r4y} the $T'=9$ entries draw
again at the second boundary into entries at $T'=8$, and those send
every type to $\mathsf{V}$ at the third. The lemma applies unchanged:
the $T'=9$ certificates price their round ends by (ii) over the $T'=8$
certificates, which price theirs over $L$. In words, \code{r1} hides
the type through one round boundary and \code{r4y} through two, and
from the third boundary on the bot plays the vertex pack in both.

\begin{remark}[A pruned export gives the bound back, 2026-11]
\label{rem:prunedexport}
The libraries of \code{r5q} draw at every boundary down to $T'=2$, and
its roots certify $0.2083$. Every entry reachable from them is
$68{,}131$ entries (\path{docs/solver/split-ladder.md}), so the store
was first exported pruned: entries whose reach fell below $3\times
10^{-6}$ were left out and every draw into them sent to $\mathsf{V}$,
which kept $22{,}889$. Recomputed bottom up from the pruned bytes, the
certificate reads $0.2212$, looser than \code{r4y}: the cut cost
$0.0129$, about ten times the price of a revealed type on the mass it
removed. The reach was the joint reach under each entry's own solve
opponent, so the cut removed what the bot holds on lines that opponent
rarely takes, and with the vertex pack there instead those lines became
the best response. A sound cut has to use the bot's own reach with the
opponent left free; stores are exported unpruned from then on.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{What is not solved.} The two root certificates of
\code{r4y} bracket the value of the game to the first bidder: it lies
in $[v_{\mathrm{lo}},v_{\mathrm{hi}}]=[-0.0922,\,+0.1275]$. The
midpoints of the brackets at successive bounds are $+0.025$ (vertex
pack), $+0.019$ (\code{r1}) and $+0.018$ (\code{r4y}), consistent with
a small edge for the first bidder; this is a reading, not a theorem.
The game is solved when that bracket closes on its value, and it is
$0.2197$ wide. Solving it would not mean a policy that wins half its
games against every opponent: the first bidder's value need not be
zero.

\paragraph{What is outstanding.} Measured, not yet done: solving the
top three layers again until the ranges they arrive with stop changing
(one such pass moved the bound by $0.011$); libraries closed under what
their own entries arrive with from $T'=5$ to $8$, where the class-prior
brackets still hold $20$ to $55\%$ of the vertex bracket; designs for
the classes whose arriving ranges no current design holds whole; the
certificate in exact arithmetic end to end, including the vertex
ladder's $25{,}222$ type solves above $T'=3$ on which every leaf rests;
an exploiter stronger than the probe, whose noise is now larger than
the gap it should measure; and a sound way to shrink a store for
serving. The game repository's \path{docs/solver/next-steps.md} (branch
\code{push/scout}) gives costs and expected gains for each.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper proved a composition theorem for safe re-solving in
heads-up Red/Black and built and verified the machinery the theorem
charges: an exact belief filter, a gadget resolver with live-measured
margins, and exact value-of-belief frontiers at the shallow layers.
Measurement located the stored profile's entire observable
exploitability in the belief reset: carrying beliefs drives the
strongest probe's lower bound from $0.598$ to $0.078\ [0.016,\,0.139]$.
The 2026-08 numbers of record extend that line to its endpoint: at
$n=10{,}000$ games per arm the belief-carrying agent reads
$+0.0342\ [+0.0146,\,+0.0538]$ over the guards-on incumbent and
$-0.0248\ [-0.0444,\,-0.0052]$ over \code{solve-6-cfrpolicy}, the
store then served, a probe reading past zero
(Remark~\ref{rem:probezero}). The bot that players face does not
re-solve, and its store since 2026-09-02, \code{profile-solve9b}, has
no probe reading on this paper's recipe (Remark~\ref{rem:served}).
Graded exactly on every class with $T'\le3$
(Section~\ref{sec:frontier-served}), that bot's store is exploitable
in the real subgame up to $0.573$ in the units of $\epsG$, most
of it the belief reset, and the exact endgame policy that replaced it
there in 2026-11 is certified by a verified checker; on the full game
the probe bounds the bot below by $\epsG\ge1.12$.
For the belief-carrying agent the certified upper bound is still the
trivial $2$, and an earlier claim of $1.958$ was rejected by audit for
substituting an on-path average for an opponent-controlled supremum.
The distance between its jaws is localized in a single quantity, the
honorability of the leaf-price surface at layers $T'\ge4$, and a
four-item program, each item motivated by a measurement in this
paper, targets exactly that quantity. A policy keyed on the class and
the seat's own type escapes that obstruction
(Section~\ref{sec:vertex}): an opponent told the type at every
boundary bounds it by $0.2998$, a floating-point composition reproduced
independently and verified exactly in its lowest layers, with the
probe at $0.263$ (SE $0.019$) below. A policy that keeps its type
hidden across boundaries, by private draws among stored round policies
each solved for a range of its own types, is bounded by $0.2197$ against
an opponent told only which policy was drawn
(Section~\ref{sec:split}); that is the bot served since 2026-11-23
12:55 Eastern, with its roots and $48$ sampled entries graded exactly
and the composition in floating point. The game is not solved: its
value to the first bidder lies in $[-0.0922,\,+0.1275]$, and closing
that bracket is the work that remains.
